# People First Leadership in a World That Wants to Forget

## The event

Bart Paden gave this talk on Monday, October 26, 2026, at Ozark Christian College in Joplin, Missouri, to about 25 students in a class called Foundations for Organizational Leadership. The talk is "People First Leadership." Students reached the talk page by scanning a QR code on the last slide. The page is archetypeoriginal.com/occ. It has the slides, Archy, and Bart's contact details.

## About Bart

Bart Paden has spent 33 years building teams. He started a company in a home office and it grew past 100 people. One idea held up the whole way, and that idea is the talk.

## The one idea

"If you grow people, you grow the organization. If you use people, you lose it." (Bart Paden)

The title says "in a world that wants to forget." Here is what the world wants to forget. People are the point. Not the output. Not the numbers. People. Bart has seen both halves of the idea. The first one held up for 33 years.

## You already know this

Think of one person who made you better. A teacher. A coach. A boss. A friend. In the talk, students turned to the person next to them and told them who it was and what they did.

What they had in common came down to three things. They paid attention to you. They believed in you before you earned it. They wanted you to win. That is servant leadership. You have been on the receiving end of it already. None of it required a title. It required attention.

## You are already leading

Somebody is looking at you the way you just described looking at that person. Group projects. Teams. Jobs. Friends who look to you. Nobody gave you a title. People follow you anyway. You choose what that looks like.

## Serving comes first

Robert Greenleaf titled his work "The Servant as Leader." Not "The Leader as Servant." Serving comes first. Leading comes second.

Jesus said it this way: "For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." (Mark 10:45)

For many years Bart's tagline has been "I am second." Even when he has to be the guy, it is not just about him. A lot of people think leadership is something you do alone. That is a heavy crown, and it is lonely at the top of that pile. Leadership is working with your team and through your team. When you see that, the weight gets lighter.

Bart thinks about the wake behind a boat. The wave behind you has consequences for everyone around you. That is what serving looks like. You pay attention to what your decisions do to the people behind you.

## Greenleaf's definition

Greenleaf spent about 40 years at AT&T. In 1970 he wrote an essay that gave this idea its name. In his words:

"The servant-leader is servant first… It begins with the natural feeling that one wants to serve, to serve first. Then conscious choice brings one to aspire to lead." (Robert K. Greenleaf, The Servant as Leader, 1970)

Notice the order. Serve first. Lead second. Notice the word choice. Nobody drifts into this. You choose it. Greenleaf said the idea came from a novel, Hermann Hesse's Journey to the East, where a servant who does the group's chores turns out to be the leader.

## Greenleaf's best test

Greenleaf gave a test. He called it the best test, and he said it is hard to administer. Do those served grow as people? Do they, while being served, become healthier, wiser, freer, more autonomous, more likely themselves to become servants? (Adapted from Robert K. Greenleaf, The Servant as Leader, 1970. Greenleaf's own word is "persons.")

That is Bart's proof statement in Greenleaf's words: if you grow people, you grow the organization. Greenleaf measured leadership by what happens to the people, not by the scoreboard.

He added one more question. What is the effect on the least privileged in society? Will they benefit, or at least not be further deprived? For students headed to the mission field, to children's ministry or to hospitals, that last question is theirs.

## The pull the other way

The world rewards scoreboards. Followers. Grades. Titles. Those things are fine. But they pull you toward using people. People first is a choice you make again and again.

## What it looks like this week

1. Learn one person's name and one thing they care about.
2. Ask a teammate how they are, then listen.
3. Give credit away.
4. Say "I was wrong" once.

That last one is the hardest. It is also the one people remember. None of this takes a title. All of it counts.

## Take this with you

Three things carried Bart: faith, curiosity and humility.

Faith. Faith was the foundation under every decision he made. Every one.

Curiosity. In 1993 there were no books about the internet. YouTube did not exist. He figured it out. You do not have to know the answer. You have to want it.

Humility. I am second. He could not do this alone, and he never did. When it failed, he owned it. When it worked, he celebrated with the people who did the work.

One question for the week: Who did I make better this week?

## The close

If you grow people, you grow the organization. You are going to lead someone soon. Make them glad they followed you.

## Questions from the discussion

1. Who did you name in the pair-share, and what did they do that you could copy this week?
2. Where do you already lead without a title?
3. Where does serving get expensive for you?
4. Does people first work in a church, a business, a hospital, a farm? Where does it break?
5. How do you lead firmly without being abrasive? Be clear about the outcome and gentle with the person.
