SECTION 7 — Part II
How ALI Works
The architecture behind the instrument
ALI works because it measures leadership the way people actually experience it —
in patterns, not personalities;
in behaviors, not beliefs;
in clarity, not charisma.
Most measurements in business focus on performance, satisfaction, or personality.
Those have their place — but none of them expose the leadership signals that determine how culture truly functions.
ALI does.
It looks at leadership through three lenses. Underneath them sit the seven conditions ALI scores: Clarity, Consistency, Accountability, Voice, Growth, Support, and Alignment.

1. Behavioral Alignment
“Does what leaders intend match what teams experience?”
This includes the signals teams feel every day:
	•	consistency
	•	fairness
	•	emotional steadiness
	•	follow-through
	•	clarity of direction
	•	clarity of expectations
	•	reliability under pressure
	•	reactive vs. responsive patterns
Behavior doesn’t lie — and people feel drift long before it becomes visible.
ALI quantifies those signals.

2. Trust and Voice
“Can people tell the truth here, and do they trust what they see?”
Trust isn't just emotional — it’s behavioral, biological, and environmental.
Zak, Edmondson, Gallup, and others proved this long ago.
ALI measures trust the way teams experience it:
	•	safety to speak honestly
	•	safety to disagree
	•	safety under mistakes
	•	predictable leadership behavior
	•	fairness across the team
	•	clarity and transparency
	•	freedom from retaliation or fear
	•	confidence in leaders under pressure
This is the core of sustainable performance.
And until now, small businesses had no way to measure it.

3. Clarity
“Do people understand what leaders think they’ve communicated?”
Most leaders believe they’re clear.
Most teams experience the opposite.
ALI doesn’t measure “communication skill.”
It measures whether the message lands:
	•	how well expectations are understood
	•	whether roles are crisp or blurred
	•	whether decisions loop or land
	•	how quickly clarity breaks under stress
	•	whether the team feels aligned or confused
	•	whether information flows or gets jammed
	•	whether leaders are communicating or offloading
Clarity is the backbone of trust, performance, and stability.
ALI exposes where it’s working — and where it’s falling apart.

 Why these three lenses matter together
Any one measurement alone gives a partial picture.
Together, they reveal:
	•	early symptoms before they become patterns
	•	patterns before they become problems
	•	problems before they become crises
This is how you protect culture:
You measure the truth before the consequences arrive.
Small businesses rarely get that chance.
ALI gives it to them.

 The ALI Question Architecture
How we avoid bias, fear, and false positives
ALI questions are designed using a structure that reduces noise and increases honesty:
1. Four wordings for every idea
Each question idea is written four equivalent ways. One of the four is reverse-worded and reverse-scored, which catches anyone who agrees with everything. Each round uses a different wording, so the survey never feels memorized while scores stay comparable. The leader and the team always get the same wording, in the same shuffled order.
2. Quarterly rounds, about 18 questions
To track movement, not moments:
	•	One round each quarter
	•	17 scored questions: two for each of the seven conditions, two for Trust, one for Commitment
	•	One unscored trial question that tests new wording
	•	Under five minutes
	•	Drift shows up from the second round on
This prevents burnout, reduces bias, and keeps the tool light for small teams.
3. No revealing which questions are “about what”
People answer differently when they know what you’re measuring.
So ALI removes the temptation.
Questions stand alone.
Weights are invisible.
Patterns are emergent.
4. No personality or preference scoring
ALI isn’t about who someone is — it’s about how leadership is experienced.
This avoids shame, ego, or defensiveness from leaders.
It keeps the focus on behavior and alignment, not identity.

 The Core Outcome of ALI
A simple question with a measurable answer:
“Is the leadership you think you’re providing
the leadership your people actually experience?”
That’s the heart of the model.
Everything else points at that one truth.
