## Scripture

**Psalm 115:16 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Some leaders hand out responsibility and keep the authority. The work is delegated. The decisions are not. Everyone on the team learns quickly that nothing is really theirs to carry, and before long they stop trying to carry it.

The psalm draws a line with real consequences. The heavens remain the LORD's heavens. The earth he has given to the children of man. Ownership is not surrendered, but the handoff is not a pretense either. Something was actually placed in human hands, with enough room to be tended well or handled badly.

That is the shape of stewardship at its healthiest. The one who gives keeps the final claim, and still gives enough that the one receiving carries real responsibility. A leader who understands that about their own position tends to extend the same pattern downward. They hand people something real, and they let it stay real.

It also removes an easy excuse. When something has genuinely been given, what happens to it next belongs to the one who received it. A leader cannot hold a real trust and then point back at the giver to explain the condition it ends up in. The giving is exactly what makes the receiving leader responsible.

The healthiest leaders hold both truths without strain. What they have was really given, so they act on it with confidence. It still belongs to someone else, so they act on it with care. Stewardship is that combination of confidence and care, sustained long enough to show up in the results.

## Practical Application

- Find one responsibility you've delegated without delegating the authority to carry it.
- Hand that authority over clearly, and say so out loud to the person carrying it.
- Look at one area entrusted to you and ask whether its current condition reflects your care.

## Takeaways

- Real stewardship involves real authority, because a trust that can't be acted on isn't a trust.
- What has genuinely been given becomes the responsibility of the one who received it.

## Closing Thought

What was truly given can be truly tended, or truly neglected.
