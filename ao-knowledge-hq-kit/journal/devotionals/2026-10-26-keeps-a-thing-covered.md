## Scripture

**Proverbs 11:13 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders get told things. Some of it is offered deliberately, some slips out in a hard moment, and some arrives because the person had nowhere else to put it. All of it is entrusted, whether or not anyone used that word.

The proverb divides people into two kinds, and the dividing line is what happens to what they were told. One goes about revealing secrets. The other is trustworthy in spirit and keeps a thing covered.

Trustworthy in spirit describes a disposition rather than a policy. It is not a leader who keeps a confidence when there was no reason to share it anyway. It is a leader who keeps it when sharing would be useful, interesting, or would win an argument in a meeting.

Most breaches are small. A detail mentioned to explain a decision. A name dropped to give an argument weight. A personal situation referenced so a team understands why somebody has been struggling. Each one feels justified in the moment, and each one teaches everybody watching exactly what their own disclosures are worth.

Stewardship of people includes stewardship of what they told you. A leader who leaks nothing gains something that cannot be requisitioned: people who bring problems early, while those problems are still small enough to solve.

## Practical Application

- Identify something you repeated that was not yours to repeat, and change the habit that made it feel acceptable.
- Practice explaining a decision without the private detail sitting behind it.
- Notice whether people bring you problems early or late, and treat the answer as information about you.

## Takeaways

- Trustworthiness here is a disposition, tested by what a leader keeps when sharing would be useful.
- People calibrate what they tell a leader based on what they have watched that leader repeat.

## Closing Thought

What people bring you early is a measure of what you have kept covered.
