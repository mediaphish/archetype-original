## Scripture

**Ephesians 4:32 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul ties the instruction to a fixed reference point instead of leaving it open to interpretation. Forgive one another, as God in Christ forgave you. The measure isn't how the offense feels today or how willing the other person seems to make it right. The measure was already set the day you were forgiven, and it doesn't move just because the current situation feels harder to excuse.

That's a difficult standard for a leader carrying real accumulated grievances. Time in leadership means time collecting legitimate reasons to withhold grace, a decision that cost the team, a promise broken, a mistake that should have been caught. Each one on its own can feel like a reasonable exception to the rule. Paul isn't interested in exceptions. The forgiveness he's describing was extended to people who had done far worse than miss a deadline or mishandle a client, and it wasn't extended in careful proportion to how sorry they seemed.

Kind and tenderhearted come before forgiving in this verse, and that ordering matters. A leader who has stopped being tenderhearted toward their team, who has hardened slightly with every accumulated frustration, will find the instruction to forgive nearly impossible by the time it's actually required. Tenderheartedness isn't a soft trait unrelated to leadership. It's the condition that makes forgiveness possible at all.

What makes this instruction demanding in practice is that it never expires. A leader doesn't forgive once and get to file the incident as resolved forever. New complaints arrive constantly, and each one gets measured against the same original standard, not against whatever patience is left over from the last time. The leader who quietly starts measuring against their own dwindling patience instead of against what they were shown has drifted from the actual instruction without noticing the drift happen.

Grace received once is easy to remember as a fact. Grace required continuously, at the scale it was originally given, is the discipline this verse is actually describing, and it's the discipline most leaders underestimate until they're the ones being asked to extend it again.

## Practical Application

- Name a current grievance you're holding against someone on your team.
- Measure it against something significant you were once forgiven, not against how justified it feels today.
- Extend forgiveness at that original scale, not at whatever patience you have left.

## Takeaways

- The standard for forgiveness was set the day you received it, and it doesn't move to match the current offense.
- Tenderheartedness that erodes under accumulated frustration leaves nothing behind for the moment forgiveness is actually required.

## Closing Thought

The measure was fixed the day it was given to you, not the day you have to give it to someone else.
