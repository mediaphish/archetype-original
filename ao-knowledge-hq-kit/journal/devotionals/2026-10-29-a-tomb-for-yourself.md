## Scripture

**Isaiah 22:15–19 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The man in question has a title and a house to run. He is named as a steward, the one over the household, which means the resources, the staff and the authority all pass through his hands. What he has done with that is cut himself a tomb in the rock.

The question God sends him is about standing. What have you to do here, and whom have you here, that you have cut out here a tomb for yourself. Twice in one verse the text says for yourself. He was given a position to run someone else's house and used it to carve his own monument into the rock.

Leaving something behind is not the problem. Every leader leaves a mark. One who builds a culture of service leaves something that outlasts them, and the household is better for it. That mark belongs to the people who stay. The tomb served no one in the house.

Stewardship is the difference. A steward asks what the position can leave for the household. Shebna asked what it could leave for him. The same access and authority can build either one.

The answer comes in the next breath, and it is not gentle. The LORD will hurl him away and thrust him from his office. He will die in a wide land, far from the tomb he carved, and the text calls him the shame of his master's house. He built something for himself, lost the household and never used what he built.

## Practical Application

- Name the marks you are leaving on your organization, and ask who each one is for.
- Ask what your title has produced for the people it was given to serve.
- Name anything you have built with your position that will leave with you rather than stay.

## Takeaways

- Every leader leaves a mark. The question is whether it was left for the household or for themselves.
- A steward can misuse a position without ever mishandling money, which is why this goes unexamined.

## Closing Thought

He carved a tomb on the height and never got to lie in it.
