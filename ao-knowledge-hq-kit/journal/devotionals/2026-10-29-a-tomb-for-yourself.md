## Scripture

**Isaiah 22:15–16 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The man in question has a title and a house to run. He is named as a steward, the one over the household, which means the resources, the staff and the authority all pass through his hands. What he has done with that is cut himself a tomb in the rock.

The tomb is personal, permanent and impossible to miss. Cut out on the height, carved into the rock, built to outlast the man and to say something about him long after he is gone. The question that comes back to him is about standing. What have you to do here, and whom have you here.

That question asks what claim he actually has on the place. A steward was handed a household to run, and he has treated the location as somewhere to establish himself permanently. Leaders do the same thing without ever touching a budget. A reputation built on the organization's platform. Relationships cultivated so they follow the person out the door. A legacy carved into the spot where the work was supposed to go.

Stewardship assumes the position is temporary and the household is the point. A steward who starts carving a legacy into the rock has already decided, whether or not they would say it out loud, that the job exists for them.

There is also the matter of who is asking. The question comes from someone with the standing to ask it, which is the part leaders tend to forget. Positions come with an owner, and owners eventually want to know what the position produced for the household rather than for the one holding it.

## Practical Application

- Name what you have built with your position that will leave with you rather than stay.
- Ask what your title has produced for the people it was given to serve.
- Separate the legacy you want from the work you were actually handed.

## Takeaways

- A steward can misuse a position without ever mishandling money, which is why this goes unexamined.
- Positions come with an owner, and owners ask what the household gained.

## Closing Thought

The tomb was impressive, and it was carved on someone else's time.
