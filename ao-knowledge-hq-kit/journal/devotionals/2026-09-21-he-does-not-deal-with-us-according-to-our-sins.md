## Scripture

**Psalm 103:8–10 (ESV)**

## Reflection

This closes the week the way it needs to close, with a direct statement of what mercy actually costs the one extending it. He does not deal with us according to our sins. The full, accurate response to what was actually done is available, and it's specifically not the response given. Mercy here isn't an absence of grounds for anger. It's a decision not to act on grounds that genuinely exist.

He will not always chide, nor will he keep his anger forever names something specific about the shape mercy takes over time. Anger itself isn't treated as illegitimate. Chiding isn't treated as always wrong. What's named is the refusal to let either one become permanent, to let a correction extend indefinitely past the point it was actually serving a purpose. A leader can hold this same distinction, correction that's real without becoming a sentence that never ends.

The whole week has been building toward this exact tension, discipline that flows from love, reproof aimed at people still cared about, correction paired with patience and teaching, pursuit of the one who's wandered, strength redirected toward gentleness. All of it depends on a leader capable of what this psalm describes, genuine grounds for a harder response, held alongside a decision not to give the full weight of it.

This is mercy stated plainly as restraint from something deserved, not the absence of anything to be angry about. A leader who extends mercy this way isn't pretending an offense didn't happen or wasn't serious. They're choosing not to repay it according to what it actually warranted, the same choice this psalm attributes to God himself.

September opened with Micah's question about what the Lord actually requires. Three weeks later, this psalm answers a related question, what the Lord actually does, having every right to deal with people according to what they deserve, and choosing something else instead.

## Practical Application

- Name something where you have full grounds for a harder response, and choose restraint deliberately, not by pretending the offense wasn't real.
- Check whether a past correction has quietly become a permanent sentence rather than something that served its purpose and ended.
- Extend the same restraint to someone this week that this psalm attributes to God.

## Takeaways

- Mercy here is restraint from a fully warranted response, not the absence of grounds for one.
- Correction is meant to end. Letting it become permanent is a different thing than the discipline this month has described.

## Closing Thought

The full weight of what was deserved was available. It simply wasn't given.
