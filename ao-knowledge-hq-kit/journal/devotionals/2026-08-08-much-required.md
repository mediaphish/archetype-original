## Scripture

**Luke 12:47–48 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Jesus closes this teaching on the faithful and unwise manager with a principle that scales rather than a rule that applies evenly. Two servants fail. Both are held accountable. But the servant who knew more carries a heavier accountability than the one who did not, because the measure of accountability is tied to what was entrusted, not just to what went wrong.

Leaders rarely think of authority this way. Authority tends to feel like a reward, a mark of trust earned, a wider platform to build from. It is also a wider account to answer for. The leader with more reach, more resources, more people depending on their decisions, is not simply more capable. They are more accountable, whether they have thought about it in those terms or not.

This is uncomfortable in a specific way. It means accountability is not a fixed standard applied equally to everyone in a room. It moves with authority. The leader with the most influence in an organization carries the most accountability in that organization, even when no one has said so out loud.

Servant leadership treats increasing responsibility as increasing accountability, not increasing exemption. The temptation as a leader gains more is to assume more latitude, more benefit of the doubt, more distance from the consequences other people face. This verse reverses that assumption entirely. What was given in trust is what will be required in full.

## Practical Application

- Name one area where your authority has grown faster than your sense of accountability for it.
- Ask what standard you would hold someone else to if they had exactly what you have been given.
- Hold yourself to that same standard starting this week.

## Takeaways

- The measure of accountability is tied to what was entrusted, not just to what went wrong.
- Increasing responsibility means increasing accountability, not increasing exemption.

## Closing Thought

What was given in trust is what will be required in full.
