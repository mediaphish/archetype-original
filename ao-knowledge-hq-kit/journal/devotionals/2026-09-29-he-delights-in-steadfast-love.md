## Scripture

**Micah 7:18–19 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Micah opens the month and closes it out in the same book, and the question asked here is worth slowing down for rather than rushing past. Who is a God like you. It isn't rhetorical filler. It's an actual claim that this particular character, pardoning iniquity while still calling it iniquity, passing over transgression without pretending it wasn't transgression, is genuinely unusual, not the default posture most people or most gods would take.

He does not retain his anger forever names the same restraint the month described back on September 21, anger with real grounds, deliberately not extended past its purpose. Delights in steadfast love goes further than simply tolerating the requirement to be merciful. It describes mercy as something actually desired, not a duty grudgingly fulfilled.

Cast all our sins into the depths of the sea is a striking image of completeness. Not filed away, not merely overlooked, but placed somewhere genuinely inaccessible, gone in a way that closes the matter rather than leaving it retrievable for later. A leader who extends mercy but keeps the offense easily reachable for future reference hasn't actually done what this verse describes.

Every passage this month has been building toward this kind of complete, willing mercy, the enemy fed rather than merely spared, the wanderer pursued rather than merely noted, the offense covered rather than merely tolerated, and now, at the end, sins genuinely put away rather than filed for later use. The month has consistently refused a version of mercy that's really just delayed judgment held in reserve.

A leader closing out this arc has to ask Micah's question honestly of their own practice. Not whether mercy was technically extended somewhere along the way, but whether it was extended the way this verse describes, completely, and with something closer to delight than obligation.

## Practical Application

- Identify an offense you extended mercy for but have kept easily reachable for future reference, and let it go completely.
- Notice whether your mercy this month has felt like an obligation or something closer to what this verse calls delight.
- Ask Micah's question of your own leadership honestly before the month closes.

## Takeaways

- Mercy that keeps the offense easily reachable for later isn't the complete mercy this verse describes.
- Delighting in steadfast love is a different posture than merely tolerating the requirement to show it.

## Closing Thought

Not filed away. Cast into the depths, genuinely gone.
