## Scripture

**Daniel 6:3–4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Daniel is distinguished above the other officials and about to be set over the whole kingdom. That also makes him the most examined man in the administration, which is how it usually works when someone is about to be promoted past their peers.

The search against him is hostile and specific. They are looking for a ground for complaint with regard to the kingdom, which means his actual work. The records, the decisions, the handling of what was entrusted to him. They find nothing, and the text gives the reason plainly. He was faithful.

Most leaders are never audited by enemies. They get audited by circumstance. A bad quarter. A lawsuit. An unexpected departure. Someone finally looking closely at a process nobody had examined in years. Faithfulness over the long stretch is what determines whether that look turns up anything.

Stewardship done well is boring under inspection. No side arrangement that needs explaining. No number that only makes sense with context. No favor that reads differently in writing than it sounded in the room.

An excellent spirit was in him sits alongside the faithfulness rather than substituting for it. Daniel was both capable and clean, and that combination is what made him impossible to remove through ordinary means.

## Practical Application

- Pick the part of your work least likely to be examined and examine it yourself.
- Find anything that would need explaining if someone read it cold, and fix the thing rather than the explanation.
- Ask whether your competence has been covering for a practice you would not want examined.

## Takeaways

- Most leaders are audited by circumstance rather than by enemies, and the result depends on what was built beforehand.
- Capability does not substitute for faithfulness, and leaders who have both are hard to dislodge.

## Closing Thought

They went looking for something and the search came back empty.
