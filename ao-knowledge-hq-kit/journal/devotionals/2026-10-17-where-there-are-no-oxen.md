## Scripture

**Proverbs 14:4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

An empty stall is easy to keep clean. Nothing to feed, nothing to shovel out, nothing that can get sick or wander off. It is also empty, which is the part the proverb is aimed at.

Abundant crops come by the strength of the ox. The cost and the capacity arrive in the same animal. A leader who wants the harvest without the mess is asking for terms the arrangement does not offer.

Leaders choose the clean manger more often than they admit. They put off hiring because people are complicated. They skip the equipment because it will need maintenance. They keep the operation small because scale brings problems they would rather not manage. Every one of those choices can be discipline, and every one of them can be avoidance wearing discipline's clothes.

Stewardship is not the same thing as keeping the place tidy. A leader given resources is expected to put them to work, and work makes a mess. The real question is never whether the manger stayed clean. It is whether the mess is producing anything.

There is a distinction worth holding onto. Some messes are waste, and a steward cleans those up. Others are the byproduct of capacity actually being used. A leader who cannot tell the two apart will keep getting rid of the ox to keep the stall clean.

## Practical Application

- Name something you have avoided because it would be messy rather than because it would be wrong.
- Sort the messes in your operation into waste and byproduct of real work.
- Take on one ox you have been avoiding, with clear eyes about what it will require.

## Takeaways

- The cost and the capacity are usually the same animal.
- A clean manger can mean discipline or avoidance, and leaders rarely stop to check which.

## Closing Thought

Nothing to feed, nothing to clean, nothing to harvest.
