## Scripture

**1 Chronicles 29:13–14 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Thanksgiving gathers people around a table to count what they have been given. For leaders it often lands near the end of a year of hard work, and it is easy to sit down feeling that most of what is on the table was earned. Some of it was. All of it was given first.

David is speaking at a moment when his people have just given freely, and the natural thing for a leader would be to take a bow. Instead he prays. And now we thank you, our God, and praise your glorious name. Then a question. But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able thus to offer willingly?

He does not ask how much they gave. He asks how they were able to give at all. Even the willingness to offer is treated as something received. For all things come from you, and of your own have we given you.

That sentence takes the credit out of generosity without taking the joy out of it. The leader who gives the most is still handing back what was put in their hands. The giving is real, and so is the gift behind it.

A leader who believes that sits at the table differently. They can be thankful for their own work without treating the harvest as their achievement, and generous without needing the recognition. Of your own have we given you is a good sentence to carry to the table, and a better one to carry into the year that follows.

## Practical Application

- Before you eat, name one thing on your table you could not have produced yourself.
- Give something away and quietly credit God for the ability to give it.
- Thank the people around you without taking credit for what they brought.

## Takeaways

- David asks not how much they gave but how they were able to give at all.
- Of your own have we given you takes the credit out of generosity without taking the joy.

## Closing Thought

Everything on the table was given before anyone gave it.
