## Scripture

**1 Corinthians 4:1–2 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul corrects how the Corinthian church has been sizing him up. They had begun ranking leaders against one another, attaching status to whoever they followed. Paul redirects the category entirely. He is not a rival brand of leadership to be preferred over another. He is a steward, managing what belongs to someone else, and the only real measure of a steward is faithfulness.

The word matters because ownership and stewardship produce different kinds of accountability. An owner answers mainly to outcomes they define for themselves. A steward answers to someone else's standard for something that was never theirs to begin with. Paul places every leader, including himself, in the second category.

Leaders slide toward ownership language without noticing. My team. My results. My vision for this organization. None of it is technically false, but all of it can quietly obscure the fact that what a leader manages, whether people, resources, or influence, was placed in their care rather than created by them. Accountability changes shape depending on which category a leader believes they are in.

Servant leadership operates from the steward's position on purpose. The accountability of a steward is not lighter than the accountability of an owner. It may be heavier, because a steward answers not only for results but for how faithfully they managed something that belonged to someone else the entire time.

## Practical Application

- Notice how often you use ownership language for things that were actually entrusted to you.
- Reframe one of them this week in steward's terms: not "my team" but "the team I am accountable for."
- Let that shift change one decision you make this week.

## Takeaways

- An owner answers mainly to outcomes they define; a steward answers to someone else's standard for something that was never theirs.
- The accountability of a steward may be heavier than the accountability of an owner, not lighter.

## Closing Thought

What was placed in your care was never yours to begin with, and the accountability follows accordingly.
