## Scripture

**John 3:27–30 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders measure themselves against other leaders constantly. Whose team is bigger. Whose voice carries further in the room. Who got the promotion, the platform, the opportunity that seemed like it should have gone the other way. Comparison is quiet, but it shapes decisions more than most leaders admit.

He must increase, but I must decrease is a sentence most leaders never say out loud. It runs against nearly every instinct a leader has been trained to follow. Protect your position. Grow your reach. Don't let anyone take the room from you.

John can say it because of where he started. Nothing he held was ever seized. All of it was given, which meant none of it was a scoreboard, and none of it had to be defended against the one it was meant to point toward. He was clear about what he was and what he was not, and he never stretched his influence to cover a role that was not his.

The surprise is joy. The friend of the bridegroom stands close enough to hear his voice and is glad, and John calls that joy complete. Most leaders can celebrate someone else's rise when it costs them nothing. Rejoicing when someone else's increase means your own decrease is only possible through stewardship, when the influence was never treated as property in the first place.

Every leader eventually faces some version of it. A successor gaining ground. A team member whose ideas now carry the room. A platform shrinking because the work it was given for is nearly done. A leader who understands that influence was given can let it go without deciding their life has lost its meaning, and can even be glad.

## Practical Application

- Name the leader you most often compare yourself to, and what that comparison has cost you.
- Identify someone whose influence should be growing while yours makes room, and help it grow.
- Be honest about what your role is not, and stop stretching your influence to cover it.

## Takeaways

- Influence cannot be seized, only received, which makes it a trust rather than a scoreboard.
- Rejoicing in another's increase at the cost of your own is only possible when influence was never held as property.

## Closing Thought

Some joy only becomes complete when someone else increases.
