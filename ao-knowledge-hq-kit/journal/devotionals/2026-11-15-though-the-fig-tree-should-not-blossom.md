## Scripture

**Habakkuk 3:17–18 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Every leader has seen a report where every line points the wrong way. Revenue down, pipeline thin, two key people gone and the bank asking questions. In a season like that, thankfulness can sound naive, like something reserved for people who have not looked at the numbers.

Habakkuk has looked at the numbers. Fig tree, vines, olives, fields, flock and herd. For a farming people that is the entire economy, every source of income named one at a time, and every one of them failing in the same sentence.

Then comes the turn. Yet I will rejoice in the LORD; I will take joy in the God of my salvation. Nothing on the list has been restored. The joy is not attached to any line item. It is attached to God, which is the only reason it can survive the list.

The verbs are decisions. I will rejoice. I will take joy. He is not describing a feeling that arrived on its own. He is choosing ahead of time what he will do if the worst happens, which is a very different thing from hoping it does not.

A leader who has settled that question before the bad quarter arrives leads differently inside it. Their team can tell the leader is worried and still steady. Thankfulness that has already been decided does not have to be renegotiated every time a number comes in, and that steadiness is often what holds a team together long enough to get through.

## Practical Application

- Write down what you would still be thankful for if every number you track went the wrong way.
- Decide now how you want to lead in a bad season, before one arrives.
- Let your team see that you can be concerned and steady at the same time.

## Takeaways

- Habakkuk names every source of income failing and still chooses joy in God.
- Thankfulness decided in advance does not have to be renegotiated with every report.

## Closing Thought

Every line on the list failed, and the yet still stood.
