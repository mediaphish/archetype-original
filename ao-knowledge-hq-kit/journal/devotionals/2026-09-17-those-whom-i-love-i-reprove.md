## Scripture

**Revelation 3:19 (ESV)**

## Reflection

This is one sentence to a church that had grown comfortable, lukewarm enough that comfort itself had become the problem. Those whom I love, I reprove and discipline. The rebuke isn't softened here, and it isn't separated from love either. Both arrive in the same breath, aimed at people who had stopped noticing how far they'd drifted.

What makes this line land differently than a general statement about discipline is the audience. This isn't correction aimed at outsiders or enemies. It's aimed specifically at people already loved, already claimed, already inside the relationship. The reproof isn't a threat to the relationship. It's evidence the relationship is still active enough to bother correcting.

A leader extending mercy to a comfortable, coasting team faces a version of this same choice. Comfort isn't automatically a problem, but comfortable enough to have stopped noticing drift is. Mercy that only ever affirms doesn't actually serve someone who has quietly settled into less than what they're capable of. Love that never reproves anything eventually stops functioning as love and starts functioning as indifference dressed up as kindness.

Be zealous and repent follows immediately after the reproof, which makes the correction purposeful rather than punitive. The rebuke isn't the end point. It's aimed at producing a specific response, renewed effort and a real change of direction. A leader who reproves without pointing toward what should happen next has only delivered half the instruction this verse models.

This is grace and mercy applied to complacency specifically, a condition that rarely responds to gentle affirmation and often only responds to someone caring enough to name it directly.

## Practical Application

- Identify someone on your team who has grown comfortable in a way that's quietly becoming a problem.
- Reprove them specifically because you're still invested in them, and let them know that's the reason.
- Point toward what should happen next, not just what's currently wrong.

## Takeaways

- Reproof aimed at someone already loved is evidence of an active relationship, not a threat to it.
- Correction without a clear direction forward is only half the instruction this verse models.

## Closing Thought

The love and the reproof arrived in the same sentence because they were never two separate things.
