## Scripture

**Luke 19:15–19 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Before the nobleman leaves to receive his kingdom, he hands each servant a mina and gives a single instruction: engage in business until I come back. When he returns, he does not ask what they preserved. He asks what they produced. The account he settles is a record of activity, not a record of safekeeping.

This distinction matters for how leaders think about accountability. It is easy to treat accountability as a defensive posture, something you satisfy by not losing what was given to you. Nothing broken, nothing missing, nothing to explain. The servants in this parable are not commended for avoiding loss. They are commended for what the entrusted resource became in their hands.

Leadership often narrows accountability down to risk management, holding steady, not being the one who caused the problem. That is real accountability, but it is not the whole of it. Genuine accountability for what a leader has been given includes what they did with it, not only what they protected it from.

Servant leadership settles its own account the way these servants did, by measuring what was made of what was entrusted. A leader who can say only that nothing was lost has met a lower bar than this parable sets. The full account includes growth, not just preservation.

## Practical Application

- List what has been entrusted to your leadership: budget, people, time, authority.
- For each one, ask whether you have merely preserved it or actually put it to work.
- Choose one area where preservation has quietly replaced production, and change that this month.

## Takeaways

- The account settled in this parable is a record of activity, not a record of safekeeping.
- A leader who can say only that nothing was lost has met a lower bar than full accountability requires.

## Closing Thought

Accountability for what you were given includes what you built with it, not only what you protected.
