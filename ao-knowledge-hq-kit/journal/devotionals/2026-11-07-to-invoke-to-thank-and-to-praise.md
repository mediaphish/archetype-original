## Scripture

**1 Chronicles 16:1–4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Plenty of organizations say they value gratitude. Few give it a job. It lives on a poster or in a yearly all-hands, and then it waits for someone to feel like it. Anything that waits for someone to feel like it tends to happen on good days and disappear on the rest.

The ark has been brought in and set inside the tent David pitched for it, and he does what good leaders do on a big day. He blesses the people and makes sure everyone receives something in their hands. All Israel, men and women, each with bread, meat and raisins.

Then he does something less obvious. He appoints some of the Levites as ministers before the ark, to invoke, to thank and to praise the LORD. Thankfulness gets a role and people assigned to it.

David did not leave thanks to whoever happened to be moved by the moment. He made it someone's work. That is how a practice becomes a culture. Someone owns it, and it happens whether or not the day was a good one.

Leaders shape what an organization keeps doing by what they assign. A celebration is a moment. An appointment outlasts the day it was made, which is why the second thing David did may matter more than the first.

## Practical Application

- Pick one form of thanks your team should practice and give it an owner.
- Look at what your organization says it values and check whether any of it has a role attached.
- Make sure the people who did the work leave with something in their hands.

## Takeaways

- Thankfulness that waits for someone to feel it happens on good days and fades on the rest.
- A leader turns a practice into a culture by assigning it.

## Closing Thought

David celebrated the day and then appointed people to keep giving thanks after it.
