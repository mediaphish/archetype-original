## Scripture

**2 Timothy 1:13–14 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul is writing to Timothy near the end of his own life, handing off something that cannot be measured in money or minas: sound teaching, tested and passed down, now entrusted to a younger leader to carry forward. The instruction is not to improve it, market it, or make it more palatable. The instruction is to guard it.

This is accountability for something intangible, and intangible things are the easiest to let slip. A budget gets audited. A title gets reviewed. But the values, the standards, and the convictions a leader was handed by someone before them rarely get checked by anyone else. The only accountability protecting a deposit like that is the leader's own commitment to keep it intact.

Leaders inherit deposits constantly: a culture built by a founder, a set of standards set by a predecessor, a reputation earned before the current leader arrived. It is possible to hold a position faithfully in every visible way while slowly letting the actual deposit erode, trading conviction for convenience one small compromise at a time.

Servant leadership treats guarding what was inherited as active accountability, not passive custody. A deposit is not guarded by leaving it untouched in a vault. It is guarded by a leader who keeps returning to it, measuring current decisions against it, and refusing to let it drift simply because drifting would be easier than defending it.

## Practical Application

- Name one value or standard you inherited from someone who led before you.
- Check whether recent decisions have quietly drifted away from it.
- Recommit to it explicitly, out loud, with the people you lead.

## Takeaways

- Intangible things like values and convictions rarely get checked by anyone else; the only accountability protecting them is the leader's own commitment.
- A deposit is not guarded by leaving it untouched; it is guarded by a leader who keeps returning to it on purpose.

## Closing Thought

What was entrusted to you in trust must be carried forward in trust, not simply left untouched.
