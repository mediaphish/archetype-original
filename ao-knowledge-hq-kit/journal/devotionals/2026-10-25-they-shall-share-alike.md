## Scripture

**1 Samuel 30:22–24 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The men who fought want the spoil limited to the men who fought. It is not an unreasonable position. They took the risk, they did the work, and the others stayed behind with the gear.

David refuses, and the refusal is not sentimental. He begins with where the spoil came from. It was given rather than earned, and he names who preserved whom. Then he sets the rule plainly. The share of the one who stays by the baggage equals the share of the one who goes down into the battle.

Every organization has baggage work. The person keeping the systems running while the team closes the deal. The one covering a shift so somebody else can travel. The support role that makes the visible role possible and never appears in the announcement.

Leaders tend to reward visibility, because visibility is what they can see without looking. Stewardship of people means deciding what the real contribution was rather than what the obvious contribution looked like, and then paying on that basis when something good gets divided.

Who would listen to you in this matter is David ending the argument rather than entertaining it. The rule becomes a statute, which is how a leader protects people who have no standing to argue for themselves.

## Practical Application

- Name the baggage work in your operation and the people doing it.
- Make sure the next reward reaches them in proportion to what they actually carried.
- Settle the rule in advance so it does not get relitigated every time something good happens.

## Takeaways

- Visibility and contribution are not the same measure, and only one of them is easy to see.
- Settling the rule in advance is how a leader protects people with no standing to argue for themselves.

## Closing Thought

The one who stayed with the baggage got the same share.
