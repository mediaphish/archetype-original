## Scripture

**Psalm 100:4–5 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Most leaders walk into the day already carrying it. The forecast, the open hire, the client who has gone quiet. By the first meeting the posture is set, and it is usually set by whatever is going wrong.

The psalm puts thanksgiving at the gate. It is how the worshiper enters, not something offered on the way out after everything has gone well. Thankfulness here is the entrance, decided before anyone knows how the day will turn.

The reason given has nothing to do with results. The LORD is good. His steadfast love endures forever, and his faithfulness reaches to all generations. Those are facts about God, which means they hold in a bad quarter as firmly as in a good one. A leader whose thankfulness depends on the numbers will only be thankful in the years the numbers cooperate.

Teams notice how a leader walks in. One who enters anxious sets an anxious room. One who enters thankful is not pretending the problems are gone. They have decided the problems are not the first thing worth naming.

So thankfulness belongs to practice rather than mood. Moods follow circumstances. Practices are chosen at the gate, every time, until they become the way a leader comes through the door.

## Practical Application

- Before your first meeting, name one thing you are thankful for that has nothing to do with results.
- Notice what you name first when you walk into a room, and whether it sets the tone you want.
- Open one regular meeting by naming something the team did well.

## Takeaways

- In the psalm, thanksgiving is how the worshiper enters, not a reward for how things went.
- Thanks grounded in who God is holds steady in years when results do not.

## Closing Thought

The gate comes before the courts, and the thanks comes before the news.
