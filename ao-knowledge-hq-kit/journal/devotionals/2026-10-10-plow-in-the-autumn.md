## Scripture

**Proverbs 20:4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Some work has a season, and the season does not wait for anyone. Plowing happens when the ground is ready, not when the one holding the plow feels like it. Miss that window and no amount of effort at harvest brings the crop back.

The sluggard in this proverb is not lazy at harvest. He shows up and seeks. The failure happened months earlier, quietly, when there was nothing to show for the work and nobody watching whether it got done.

Leadership is full of autumn work. Hiring before the crisis. Writing down the process while everyone still remembers it. Training a successor while there is time to do it well. Having the hard conversation while it is still small enough to have. None of it produces anything visible on the day it happens.

The discipline is spending time in the right season rather than the loudest one. A leader who works only on what is urgent will always be seeking at harvest, puzzled that the results are not there, because the season that would have produced them went by unused. That is stewardship measured in seasons rather than hours.

The proverb does not threaten anyone. It describes a sequence. What was not plowed cannot be harvested, and the gap between those two moments is long enough that most leaders have forgotten what caused the shortage by the time they feel it.

## Practical Application

- Name the autumn work in front of you that will not show a result for months.
- Give it a date and defend that date the way you would defend a customer deadline.
- Take a shortage you are dealing with now and trace it back to the season it was created in.

## Takeaways

- Work with a season attached cannot be recovered by effort later.
- A leader's most consequential work rarely shows a result on the day it gets done.

## Closing Thought

The harvest was decided months before anyone went looking for it.
