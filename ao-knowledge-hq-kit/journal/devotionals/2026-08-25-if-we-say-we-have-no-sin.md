## Scripture

**1 John 1:8 (ESV)**

## Reflection

John does not describe the person who denies wrongdoing as mistaken about a few details. He says something sharper. That person deceives themselves, and the truth is not in them. The problem is not a gap in information. It is a posture toward the self that will not admit fault as a category.

This is the quietest way to avoid accountability, because it requires no confrontation with anyone else. A leader who insists, even privately, that they have no real fault to answer for has removed the entire premise accountability depends on. There is nothing to give an account of if there was never anything wrong.

Leadership makes this posture easier to sustain than almost any other role does. People soften feedback before it reaches a leader's desk. Wins get attributed to vision, losses get attributed to circumstance. A leader can go a long time without anyone directly telling them they were at fault, and the absence of correction can start to feel like the absence of fault.

John's language is deliberately blunt because the stakes of this particular self-deception are high. It does not just distort a single decision. It removes the leader's own ability to see themselves accurately, which is the one thing every other form of accountability depends on. A leader cannot answer honestly for what they will not admit they did.

The alternative John points toward is not self-condemnation. It is simple honesty about the category itself: that leaders, like everyone else, have real fault to account for, not as an occasional exception but as the ordinary condition. A leader who accepts that starting point is far more equipped to hear correction than one who has quietly decided they are the exception.

## Practical Application

- Ask someone who reports to you directly what feedback they have been softening before it reaches you.
- Notice any pattern of attributing failures only to circumstances and successes only to yourself.
- Practice naming one specific fault out loud, to yourself or to someone else, without qualifying it.

## Takeaways

- Denying fault as a category removes the premise accountability depends on. There is nothing to answer for if nothing was ever wrong.
- Leadership makes self-deception easier to sustain, because correction reaches a leader's desk less often than it should.

## Closing Thought

The truth is not in the leader who has decided there is nothing left to admit.
