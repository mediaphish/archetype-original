## Scripture

**2 Timothy 4:2 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul lists three different modes of correction here, reprove, rebuke, and exhort, and then qualifies all three with the same phrase, complete patience and teaching. The instruction isn't to pick the gentlest available option. It's to hold the full range, from the mildest correction to the most direct rebuke, inside a posture of patience the whole time.

That combination is harder to sustain than either half alone. A leader can manage patience toward people who don't need much correcting. A leader can manage direct rebuke toward people they've run out of patience for. What Paul describes is patience that survives all the way through the harder end of the range, still present even when the correction required is rebuke rather than gentle exhortation.

In season and out of season removes the leader's ability to wait for a convenient moment. Correction doesn't get scheduled for when it's easy to deliver graciously. It has to be ready whenever it's actually needed, which means the patience underneath it has to be a standing condition, not something summoned only when the timing is favorable.

Teaching sits alongside patience as the second qualifier, which suggests correction that isn't teaching something is incomplete no matter how patiently it's delivered. A leader can be endlessly patient while still failing to actually instruct, leaving the person corrected but no more equipped than before. Mercy shown through correction has to include the teaching, not just the tone.

This is the fullest picture the week offers of what grace paired with a real standard actually requires in practice, patience broad enough to cover every mode of correction, and teaching thorough enough that the correction leaves someone better equipped, not just chastened.

## Practical Application

- Notice whether your patience holds up equally well across mild correction and direct rebuke, or only the easier end.
- Prepare to correct someone even when the timing feels inconvenient, rather than waiting for an easier moment.
- Add actual instruction to your next correction, not just the correction itself.

## Takeaways

- Patience is asked to cover the full range from gentle correction to direct rebuke, not just the easier end of it.
- Correction that doesn't teach something leaves a person chastened but no better equipped than before.

## Closing Thought

The patience was never meant to wait for a convenient season.
