## Scripture

**Genesis 4:9–10 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Cain answers a direct question with a question of his own. God asks where Abel is. Cain asks whether he is responsible for him at all.

That second question is the real subject of the passage. Cain is not simply lying about what happened. He is disputing whether accountability for another person was ever his to carry.

Leaders ask a version of that question constantly, usually without saying it out loud. Was I accountable for what happened to that employee. Was that outcome mine to answer for. The people under a leader's care did not stop being the leader's accountability the moment things went wrong for them.

Servant leadership answers Cain's question before it is asked. Yes. The people entrusted to a leader remain the leader's accountability, in success and in failure. That accountability does not transfer to the person who struggled just because the leader would rather it did.

God's response to Cain names the cost of avoiding that accountability. The blood cries out. Ignoring what happened to someone under your care does not make the outcome disappear. It shows up later, in turnover, in trust that quietly erodes, in a team that stops believing the leader will stand behind them.

## Practical Application

- Name one person currently under your care whose outcome you have mentally distanced yourself from.
- Ask honestly whether that distance is accurate or convenient.
- Reclaim accountability for what is actually yours to carry.

## Takeaways

- The people entrusted to a leader remain the leader's accountability, in success and in failure.
- Accountability that is refused does not disappear. It surfaces somewhere else.

## Closing Thought

Accountability that goes unclaimed does not vanish. It resurfaces where it is least expected.
