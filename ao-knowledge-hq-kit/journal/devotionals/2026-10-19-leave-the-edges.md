## Scripture

**Leviticus 19:9–10 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The law does not ask for a donation once the harvest has been counted. It tells the owner not to harvest all of it in the first place. The margin stays standing in the field rather than being transferred later out of whatever surplus survived the year.

Do not reap your field right up to its edge. Do not strip the vineyard bare. The yield gets reduced at the source, deliberately, and the reason is stated without decoration: leave them for the poor and for the sojourner.

That runs against the instinct most leaders are trained into. Optimization says take everything available and decide afterward what to give back. This builds the giving into the design, where it no longer depends on how generous a leader feels after a hard quarter.

Stewardship here includes what a leader deliberately does not take. Margin left for a struggling supplier. Hours not billed. Capacity held back so someone can learn by doing the work. Room in a schedule for people who move slower than the plan would prefer.

The edges are not waste, and they are not quite charity either. They are part of the field, left standing on purpose, for people who need a way to work for what they receive rather than ask for it.

## Practical Application

- Find one place where you harvest right up to the edge, and leave something standing.
- Build one act of generosity into a process instead of relying on your goodwill at year end.
- Hold back capacity so someone can learn by doing work you could finish faster yourself.

## Takeaways

- Generosity built into the design does not depend on how a leader feels when the year has been hard.
- What a leader deliberately does not take is part of stewardship.

## Closing Thought

The edges were part of the field, left standing on purpose.
