## Scripture

**1 Timothy 3:4–5 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul is listing qualifications for overseers, and he places this one in an unexpected location: not in a person's public competence, but in their household. Before Paul asks whether someone can lead an organization, he asks whether they can lead the people closest to them, the ones who see them every day with no audience and no performance required.

This is a different test of accountability than the ones this week has covered so far. It is not about tending to people already under a leader's formal authority. It is about whether a person has demonstrated accountability to people in the smallest, least visible sphere available to them, before anyone hands them a larger one. Paul's logic is direct: the skills required to care well for a household are the same skills required to care well for anything larger. If they are absent at home, there is no reason to expect them to appear at scale.

Leaders sometimes treat their most visible responsibilities as the real test of their leadership and their closest relationships as a separate, lower-stakes category. This passage rejects that separation. The household is not a distraction from leadership. It is the first and most honest evidence of it, precisely because it is the place with the least incentive to perform.

Servant leadership does not wait for a title to begin practicing accountability to those closest to it. A leader who manages a team well while their household quietly suffers has not actually passed the test this passage describes. They have only passed the version of it that has an audience.

## Practical Application

- Consider whether your leadership at home matches the leadership people see from you publicly.
- Identify one place where the gap between the two is widest.
- Address that gap before adding anything new to your public responsibilities.

## Takeaways

- Paul places the test of leadership accountability in the household first, because it is the sphere with the least incentive to perform.
- A leader who manages a team well while their household suffers has only passed the version of the test that has an audience.

## Closing Thought

Accountability to those you lead in public is only as real as the accountability you already practice at home.
