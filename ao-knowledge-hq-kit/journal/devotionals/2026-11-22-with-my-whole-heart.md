## Scripture

**Psalm 9:1–2 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Most leaders keep two kinds of records. The problems are tracked in detail, with dates, owners and follow-up. The good things that happened get a line in a year-end speech, if that. Over time the detailed record starts to feel like the true one, and gratitude starts to feel like an exaggeration.

David starts the other way. I will give thanks to the LORD with my whole heart; I will recount all of your wonderful deeds. Whole heart means nothing held back or kept in reserve in case things turn. Recount means going through them one at a time, the way a careful person counts inventory.

All of your wonderful deeds is a large claim, and it requires paying attention. Nobody can recount what they never noticed. Thankfulness of this kind depends on a leader keeping as close a watch on what God has done as they keep on what is going wrong.

Then comes what the counting produces. I will be glad and exult in you; I will sing praise to your name, O Most High. The gladness follows the recounting. It does not come from a better quarter. It comes from a record that was finally kept with the same care as the other one.

Leaders who are always tired and slightly grim are often working from only one ledger. A leader who is thankful with a whole heart, and can name specifics, gives the people around them a fuller picture of what is true, and people decide better when they can see all of it.

## Practical Application

- Keep a written record of what God has done, with the same detail you give to problems.
- Recount three specific things before your next planning meeting.
- Notice whether your team hears more about what is wrong than about what has been given.

## Takeaways

- Recounting all of God's wonderful deeds requires noticing them in the first place.
- Gladness follows a record that is kept with care.

## Closing Thought

David counted the wonderful deeds one at a time, and the gladness came after.
