## Scripture

**Psalm 116:12–14 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders make a lot of commitments. To a team about how they will be treated. To a board about what will get done. To a family about being home for dinner. Most of them are made sincerely, and plenty of them quietly slide.

What shall I render to the LORD for all his benefits to me. It is the question a thankful person eventually asks, and it has no good answer if it is taken as a bill. Nobody pays back what was given here. The psalm does not try. It answers with three actions, and none of them settles a debt.

The last of the three is the most practical. I will pay my vows. Vows are promises already made, and the response is to keep them. Not a new promise, not a grand gesture. Doing what was already said.

Thankfulness that lives only in words will eventually sit next to a broken promise, and people believe the broken promise. A leader who thanks a team warmly and then misses the commitment made to that same team has told them which one counts.

In the presence of all his people is not a throwaway line. Kept promises are visible. A leader who answers the question of what to render by keeping their word in public shows everyone watching what gratitude looks like when it has a job to do.

## Practical Application

- List the commitments you have made to your team and check which ones are still being kept.
- Keep one promise this week that you have been letting slide.
- Let your thanks show up as follow-through rather than another speech.

## Takeaways

- What God has given cannot be paid back, but it can be answered.
- Kept promises are the most visible form a leader's thankfulness takes.

## Closing Thought

The psalm's answer to what to give back is to keep what was already promised.
