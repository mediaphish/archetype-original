## Scripture

**Psalm 92:1–4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders tend to have a morning routine and an evening review. The morning is for the list. The evening is for what went wrong and what has to be fixed tomorrow. Both are useful, and both point attention at problems.

The psalm keeps the same two hours and fills them differently. Steadfast love declared in the morning, before the day has delivered anything. Faithfulness declared at night, after the day has had every chance to disprove it.

The morning declaration is trust. The evening one is testimony. One says what a leader expects to find, and the other reports what was actually there. A leader who keeps both starts to notice how often the evening confirms the morning.

The psalm opens with a plain claim. It is good to give thanks to the LORD. It does not wait for a feeling to arrive first. Thankfulness is treated as something worth doing on purpose, and anything done on purpose can be done on a schedule.

The gladness comes from the work of God's hands, not the leader's. That takes some weight off. Nobody needs a great day to end it thankful. They only need an honest look at what was given in it.

## Practical Application

- Before you open your list in the morning, name one way you expect to see God's faithfulness.
- When the day is done, look for where it showed up before you look for what went wrong.
- Keep both hours for a month and see how often the evening confirms the morning.

## Takeaways

- The morning declaration is trust, and the evening one is testimony.
- A leader does not need a great day to end it thankful, only an honest look at what was given.

## Closing Thought

The day gets two declarations, one before it proves anything and one after.
