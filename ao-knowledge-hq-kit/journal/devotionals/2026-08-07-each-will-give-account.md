## Scripture

**Romans 14:10–12 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul has spent this section of Romans addressing believers who were quick to judge one another over disputable matters, what to eat, which days to observe. Before he tells them to stop, he reminds them why the judging was never theirs to do. Everyone will stand before the judgment seat of God. Each will give an account of himself.

That last phrase is the turn. Not an account of someone else. Not an account of a team, a decision made by committee, or a culture a leader inherited. An account of himself. The accountability Paul describes cannot be delegated, shared, or diffused across an organization chart.

Leaders spend most of their working lives managing collective accountability, whose team missed the target, which department owns the failure, how responsibility gets distributed fairly across a group. That work matters. But underneath it sits a category of accountability that never becomes collective. What a leader personally chose to believe, to overlook, to excuse in themselves, remains theirs alone to answer for.

Servant leadership holds both categories without confusing them. A leader is accountable for the people and outcomes entrusted to them, and separately, irreducibly, accountable for who they personally were inside every one of those decisions. The second kind cannot be satisfied by pointing to the first. No amount of organizational success covers a personal account left unexamined.

Paul's conclusion, that each of us will give an account of himself to God, is not a threat aimed at silencing the judgmental believers he was correcting. It is the reason judging others was always beside the point. Everyone carries a full account of their own that no one else can carry for them.

## Practical Application

- Separate your accountability for outcomes your team owns from your accountability for choices only you made.
- Identify one personal choice this month you have been folding into a team's collective result instead of owning directly.
- Give yourself the same honest account you would expect from someone reporting to you.

## Takeaways

- The accountability Paul describes cannot be delegated, shared, or diffused across an organization chart.
- No amount of organizational success covers a personal account left unexamined.

## Closing Thought

Accountability owed for one's self is a debt no amount of leadership can transfer.
