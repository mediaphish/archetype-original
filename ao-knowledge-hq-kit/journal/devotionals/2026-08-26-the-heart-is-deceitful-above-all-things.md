## Scripture

**Jeremiah 17:9–10 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Jeremiah asks a question he does not expect anyone to answer well. Who can understand the heart. Not who can control it, or improve it. Who can even understand it. He has already told us it is deceitful above all things, and desperately sick, before asking whether anyone can see clearly into it.

The verse that follows is the real turn. The Lord searches the heart and tests the mind, to give every man according to his ways. The very thing a person cannot reliably read in themselves is precisely what will be examined and answered for. Self-assessment is disqualified as the final court, and accountability moves to someone with clearer sight.

This matters because so much avoidance runs entirely on internal testimony. A leader tells themselves they meant well, that their motives were sound, that they would know if something were seriously wrong inside them. Jeremiah's warning is that this internal witness is the least reliable one available, precisely because it is the one doing the deceiving.

A leader who has absorbed this will stop treating their own sense of integrity as sufficient proof of it. They will actively seek outside examination rather than resist it, because they know the heart that tells them everything is fine is the same heart Jeremiah calls deceitful. This is not paranoia. It is realism about a specific kind of blind spot.

Accountability, in this light, is not primarily about getting caught by others. It is about accepting that the search Jeremiah describes is already happening, and that the internal alibi every person is tempted to trust will not hold up under it. The leader who welcomes real examination now is simply agreeing early with what is already true.

## Practical Application

- Identify one area where you have been relying on your own sense of good intentions as proof enough.
- Invite someone you trust to examine a decision you made recently, rather than explaining it away yourself.
- Hold your own internal account of events with the same scrutiny you would apply to anyone else's.

## Takeaways

- The heart's own testimony about itself is the least reliable evidence available, which is exactly why it should never be the final word.
- Real accountability accepts outside examination rather than resisting it, because the internal witness cannot be fully trusted.

## Closing Thought

The heart that insists everything is fine is the same heart Jeremiah calls deceitful above all things.
