## Scripture

**Isaiah 40:10–11 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The might described in verse 10 and the gentleness described in verse 11 belong to the same figure, arriving back to back with no tension between them. A leader who comes with might rules with strength, and the very next line has that same leader carrying lambs against his chest and slowing his pace for the ones who are with young. Strength and tenderness aren't competing here. They're the same character shown from two angles, the same way Exodus 34 named mercy and justice together earlier this month.

Gently lead those that are with young is a specific kind of mercy, adjusted pace rather than blanket accommodation. The shepherd doesn't slow down for everyone equally. He slows down specifically for those who can't keep the normal pace, while presumably still leading the flock forward as a whole. Mercy here looks like reading who actually needs the adjustment, not applying the same treatment indiscriminately to everyone.

Carrying the lambs in his arms is different again, not adjusted pace but direct help for those who can't walk the distance at all yet. A leader extending mercy has to be capable of both responses, slowing the pace for some and outright carrying others, and knowing which one a given person actually needs.

This image completes something the whole month has been building toward. A leader who holds real strength, real authority, real capacity to rule with might, still bends that strength toward gentleness for the vulnerable specifically. The might isn't withheld to make room for gentleness. It's the same might redirected toward carrying rather than driving.

Mercy shown this way isn't softness substituting for strength. It's strength that has decided what it's for.

## Practical Application

- Identify who on your team needs a slower pace right now, and who needs to actually be carried through something.
- Notice whether you're applying the same expectation to everyone regardless of what they're currently capable of.
- Use your actual strength or authority this week specifically on behalf of someone who can't keep up right now.

## Takeaways

- Strength and gentleness are shown as the same character, not as competing instincts a leader has to choose between.
- Mercy requires reading who needs a slower pace and who needs to be carried outright, not one uniform response.

## Closing Thought

The might was never withheld to make room for the gentleness. It was redirected toward it.
