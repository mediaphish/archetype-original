## Scripture

**3 John 1:1–4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders put years into people. Coaching, correcting, modeling and hoping something sticks. The real test rarely happens while the leader is watching. It happens after the leader has moved on, in rooms they will never be in.

The elder writes to Gaius, a man he loves in truth, and tells him why he is glad. Some brothers came and testified to your truth. The report about Gaius came secondhand, from people who had seen him when the elder had not.

I rejoiced greatly. I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth. Not greater than a successful mission or a growing following. No greater joy at all. That is a leader whose thankfulness is attached to people more than to results.

Notice the word walking. It is ongoing. The elder is not glad that Gaius once agreed with him. He is glad that Gaius keeps living it, day after day, with nobody grading the work.

Leaders who want this kind of joy have to invest in people long before any report could come back. And when the report comes, they should say so out loud. Telling someone you heard how they handled something when you were not there is one of the most powerful kinds of thanks a leader can give.

## Practical Application

- Tell someone you developed that you heard how they handled something without you.
- Ask yourself what report you hope comes back about your people years from now.
- Invest now in someone whose report you may never get to hear.

## Takeaways

- The truest test of what a leader built happens when the leader is not there.
- A leader thankful for people more than results has found a deeper kind of joy.

## Closing Thought

The best news the elder could get was a report about someone else.
