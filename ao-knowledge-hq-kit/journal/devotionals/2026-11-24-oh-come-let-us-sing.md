## Scripture

**Psalm 95:1–2 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders spend most of their day telling people what to do. Assignments, deadlines, priorities. It becomes so natural that even good things come out as instructions. Be grateful. Celebrate the win. Take a moment to appreciate the team. Spoken as a directive, thankfulness turns into one more item on someone's to-do list.

The psalm opens differently. Oh come, let us sing to the LORD; let us make a joyful noise to the rock of our salvation! Let us. The one speaking is not standing outside the group giving orders. He is inside it, inviting everyone to come along, and he is going too.

Let us come into his presence with thanksgiving. The thanks is something the whole group does together. Nobody is assigned to be thankful on behalf of everyone else, and the person issuing the invitation does not get to sit it out.

The other word worth noticing is noise. A joyful noise, twice. Nobody is grading the performance. Thankfulness offered together does not need to be polished to be real, and a leader who waits until it can be done well may never invite anyone at all.

Teams learn thankfulness from leaders who practice it alongside them, not from leaders who recommend it. An invitation that begins with let us costs a leader their place above the group for a moment. It gives them a place inside it, which is usually where the real leading happens.

## Practical Application

- Replace one instruction with an invitation that includes you.
- Give thanks out loud with your team, without waiting until it sounds polished.
- Join in when your team celebrates, instead of watching from the side.

## Takeaways

- Let us puts the leader inside the group that gives thanks.
- A joyful noise does not need to be polished to be real.

## Closing Thought

The psalm says let us, and the one who says it comes too.
