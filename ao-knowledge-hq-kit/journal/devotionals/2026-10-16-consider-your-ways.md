## Scripture

**Haggai 1:2–6 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Nobody in this passage is idle. They have sown, eaten, drunk, clothed themselves and earned wages. Effort is not the problem. Where the effort went, and what it produced, is the problem.

Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins. The contrast is between a finished private comfort and an unfinished shared obligation. Leaders know that trade intimately. The leader's own office, title, equipment and travel get sorted out, while the thing everyone depends on keeps waiting for next quarter.

Consider your ways is given as an instruction rather than a scolding to absorb and move past. The evidence comes right behind it. Sown much, harvested little. Eating without being satisfied. Wages going into a bag with holes. Real resources were spent and produced less than they should have.

That is a stewardship question before it is a spiritual one. When output keeps falling short of input, something is misallocated, and leaders tend to respond by pushing harder on the same arrangement instead of examining whether the arrangement is the thing that is broken.

A bag with holes is a precise picture. The money was genuinely earned. It did not stay. Any leader can name their own version of that if they are willing to look at where resources actually go rather than where the plan says they go.

## Practical Application

- Compare what you spent over the last quarter against what it actually produced.
- Find the private comfort that got finished while something shared stayed unfinished.
- Name one bag with holes in your operation and close it.

## Takeaways

- When output keeps falling short of input, allocation is the issue rather than effort.
- Leaders usually answer diminishing returns by pushing harder on the same arrangement.

## Closing Thought

The wages were real. The bag had holes.
