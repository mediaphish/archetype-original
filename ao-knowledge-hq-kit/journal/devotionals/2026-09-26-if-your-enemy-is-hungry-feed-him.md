## Scripture

**Romans 12:19–20 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul doesn't ask for passive restraint here. Never avenge yourselves is the easier half of the instruction, and most leaders can manage it, at least outwardly. To the contrary, if your enemy is hungry, feed him, is the harder half, active provision rather than mere non-retaliation. The instruction moves past simply not harming the enemy into actually meeting their needs.

This is different from every prior passage this week in one specific way. It's not asking for an internal posture, prayer, or a single dramatic gesture. It's asking for ordinary, practical care extended specifically to someone hostile. Feed him. Give him something to drink. These are the same basic acts of care a leader would extend to anyone struggling, deliberately redirected toward the person working against them.

Heaping burning coals on his head is a strange enough phrase that it's worth pausing on rather than skimming past. Whatever the exact ancient image behind it, the effect described is clear enough. Genuine kindness extended to someone hostile produces something in them that hostility never could, a kind of exposure or discomfort that comes from being met with grace instead of the retaliation they may have expected or even wanted to provoke.

A leader with a genuine adversary, someone actively working against their interests, faces a real choice about what kind of provision they're willing to extend. Withholding basic decency toward that person can feel justified, even principled. This passage removes that justification specifically, replacing it with ordinary care extended on purpose to the person least likely to have earned it by any standard measure.

This is grace and mercy in its most practical form all month, not a feeling, not even a single costly act, but the same everyday provision a leader would offer anyone, deliberately not withheld from an enemy.

## Practical Application

- Identify a genuine adversary whose basic needs you'd normally feel justified ignoring.
- Extend one concrete act of ordinary provision to them this week, not a grand gesture, just basic decency.
- Notice whether withholding kindness from this person has started to feel principled rather than what it actually is.

## Takeaways

- Never avenging yourself is the easier half of this instruction. Actively providing for an enemy is the harder, more specific one.
- Ordinary provision extended to someone hostile does something retaliation never could.

## Closing Thought

The instruction was never just to withhold harm. It was to actually feed him.
