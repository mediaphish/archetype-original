## Scripture

**Matthew 5:43–44 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Jesus doesn't introduce this teaching quietly. He states the common assumption first, love your neighbor and hate your enemy, then directly overturns it. Everything the month has covered so far, mercy toward a team member, mercy toward someone who wronged you personally, mercy that corrects rather than excuses, has stayed inside a relationship where some baseline of goodwill already existed. This verse removes that baseline entirely.

An enemy, by definition, is someone actively working against you. Nothing about that relationship suggests grace is owed or expected. That's precisely why this instruction is placed here, not because enemies deserve mercy more than anyone else, but because they're the test case where a leader's actual capacity for mercy gets revealed rather than assumed.

Pray for those who persecute you goes further than simply tolerating an enemy or refusing retaliation. Prayer requires actually holding the person in mind, wishing something for them rather than merely refraining from wishing something against them. A leader who has an active enemy and prays for them has moved past management of the relationship into something that actually resembles the mercy this month has been describing all along.

This instruction doesn't ask a leader to pretend the opposition isn't real or stop protecting what needs protecting. It asks for something to happen internally alongside whatever external response the situation genuinely requires, a posture toward the person that doesn't calcify into hatred just because the relationship is adversarial.

The rest of this week walks through people who actually lived this instruction out, not as a theory but as a specific, costly choice made in a specific moment. This verse is the standard the rest of the week gets measured against.

## Practical Application

- Name someone you'd genuinely classify as working against you, not just someone who's difficult.
- Pray specifically for that person this week, not just for the situation to resolve.
- Notice whether your internal posture toward them has calcified into something permanent, and choose to loosen it.

## Takeaways

- Mercy toward someone with no baseline goodwill is where a leader's actual capacity for it gets revealed.
- Praying for an enemy requires holding them in mind, not just refraining from retaliation.

## Closing Thought

Everything else this month assumed some relationship worth preserving. This one doesn't, and it's asked for anyway.
