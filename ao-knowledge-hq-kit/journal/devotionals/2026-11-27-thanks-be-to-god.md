## Scripture

**1 Corinthians 15:55–58 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders measure whether work mattered by whether it produced something they can see. A project that ships, a client that stays, a number that moves. When the results do not show up, steadiness gets hard to sustain, and many leaders quietly stop putting in the effort they used to.

Paul has been writing about death. O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting? The sting of death is sin. Then the turn. But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ. The victory is over death itself, and it has already been given.

Then the word that connects everything. Therefore. Be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord. The steadiness is not built on how the work is going. It is built on a victory that was settled before the work began.

Knowing that in the Lord your labor is not in vain. Paul does not promise that every labor will produce visible results. He says it is not in vain, which is a different claim, and one a leader can hold onto through the years when the results are thin.

Thankfulness for what has already been won changes how a leader works on what is unfinished. They do not need each project to prove that their effort counts. A leader who starts from thanks be to God can keep showing up steadfast and immovable after the applause has stopped.

## Practical Application

- Thank God for the outcome that is already settled before you look at the ones that are not.
- Keep doing faithful work in one area where results have been slow.
- Remind someone discouraged on your team that their labor is not in vain.

## Takeaways

- Thanks for the victory comes first, and steadfastness follows it.
- Labor in the Lord is not in vain, even when the results are thin.

## Closing Thought

The victory came first, and therefore the work could keep going.
