## Scripture

**Psalm 69:29–30 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders learn early to keep pain out of their voice. The diagnosis, the divorce, the deal that cost them personally all get filed away before the Monday meeting. Some of that is wisdom. Some of it turns into a habit of pretending, and pretending eventually leaks into everything else, including gratitude.

The psalmist does not pretend. But I am afflicted and in pain. No qualifications and no attempt to make it sound better than it is. Then a request that has not been answered yet. Let your salvation, O God, set me on high.

The very next line is thanks. I will praise the name of God with a song; I will magnify him with thanksgiving. Nothing has changed between the two verses. The affliction is still there. The thanksgiving does not wait for the pain to leave the room.

That is what makes it believable. Thankfulness that only shows up after the pain is gone can look like relief. Thankfulness spoken while the pain is still present has nothing to gain from the circumstances. It is aimed entirely at God.

Nobody has to choose between honesty and thankfulness. A leader who can say this is hard and I am thankful in the same breath gives their people permission to do the same. Teams that only ever see a leader's gratitude on good days learn that thanks is something you perform once things are fine.

## Practical Application

- Name one hard thing you are carrying right now, honestly, to God or to someone you trust.
- Give thanks in the same prayer, without waiting for the hard thing to resolve.
- Let a trusted person on your team see both your honesty and your thankfulness.

## Takeaways

- The psalm moves from afflicted and in pain to thanksgiving, with nothing resolved in between.
- A leader does not have to choose between honesty and thankfulness.

## Closing Thought

The pain was still in the first verse when the thanksgiving arrived in the second.
