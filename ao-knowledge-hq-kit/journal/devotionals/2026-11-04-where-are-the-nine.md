## Scripture

**Luke 17:12–19 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Every leader has a list of people who helped and never heard about it. A mentor who opened a door. An early client who took a chance. An employee who covered for a mistake years ago. The help was received, the road kept going, and turning back never quite made the list.

Ten were cleansed here, all of them on the road, doing what they had been told. Nine kept walking. None of the nine did anything wrong, which is exactly what makes them familiar to any leader.

The one who came back had to stop, turn around and retrace the road. His thankfulness cost him time and distance the others did not spend. He came back loud, praising God, and fell on his face at Jesus' feet. There was nothing efficient about it.

Jesus notices the gap. Were not ten cleansed. Where are the nine. He counts who returned, and the count is one. Whatever the other nine thought about what happened, they are not in the count.

The one who returned was the one the text marks as a foreigner, the person nobody in the story would have picked. Leaders who practice turning back often find that the people they thank did not expect it either, and that is a large part of why it lands.

## Practical Application

- Name someone who helped you years ago and has never heard what it meant. Go back to them.
- Make the thanks specific enough that they know exactly what they did.
- Notice who on your team turns back, and make sure it does not go unnoticed.

## Takeaways

- Receiving and returning are different acts, and only one of them costs anything.
- Thanks that never makes the trip back never reaches the person it is about.

## Closing Thought

All ten were healed on the road, and only one turned around.
