## Scripture

**Galatians 6:1 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Two instructions arrive back to back in this single verse, and most leaders are noticeably better at the first than the second. Restore the one who's failed, in a spirit of gentleness. That part gets preached often and practiced fairly well. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted. That half tends to disappear the moment the first half is underway.

Paul isn't tacking on a caveat here as an afterthought. The instruction to watch yourself sits inside the same sentence as the instruction to restore gently, which suggests they're not two separate tasks but one posture with two parts. A leader who restores someone without any accompanying awareness of their own exposure to the same failure isn't following half the instruction well. They're following it in a way the verse doesn't actually describe.

There's a particular kind of harshness that shows up in correction, and it usually comes from a leader who has quietly filed themselves under a different category than the person who failed. Not tempted the way they were. Not capable of the same lapse. That distance makes correction easier to deliver and easier to deliver without much mercy attached, because the leader has stopped seeing themselves as someone who could end up needing the same gentleness someday.

Gentleness offered to someone caught in failure is not the soft option compared to strict correction. It's what a leader extends when they haven't forgotten, even for the length of one conversation, that they are not exempt from the same category of failure. The moment a leader believes they're the exception is the exact moment they've stopped watching themselves the way this verse requires.

This closes the week the way it opened, with mercy stated as a requirement rather than a favor. What Micah said on the first day and what Paul says here land in the same place. The leader doesn't get to decide mercy is optional once the standard has been met. It's built into how the standard gets applied to real people.

## Practical Application

- The next time you correct someone, notice your own tone before you notice your words.
- Ask honestly what you're capable of, not just what you've managed to avoid so far.
- Restore someone this week the way you'd want to be restored if the failure had been yours.

## Takeaways

- Restoring gently and watching your own exposure to the same failure are one instruction, not two.
- A leader who believes they're the exception has already stopped watching themselves.

## Closing Thought

Gentleness toward someone else's failure and honesty about your own are the same posture.
