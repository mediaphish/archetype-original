## Scripture

**1 Samuel 25:32–35 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Most leaders can recall a decision they were moments away from making in anger. The email drafted and ready, the call they were about to place, the person they were about to cut loose. Some of those got sent. A few were stopped by someone with the nerve to step in.

David was on his way to wipe out Nabal's household when Abigail hurried to meet him. His response is thanks, aimed in two directions. Blessed be the LORD, who sent you this day to meet me. Blessed be your discretion, and blessed be you.

He names exactly what she prevented. You have kept me this day from bloodguilt and from working salvation with my own hand. There is no softening it. David is thankful for being stopped, and he says plainly what he was about to do.

Then he acts on it. He receives what she brought, sends her home in peace and says, I have obeyed your voice. The thanks is not a speech before he goes back to his original plan. It changes what he does next.

Leaders who are thankful for the person who stopped them make it safe for the next person to step in. Leaders who resent being stopped teach everyone around them to stay out of the road. The difference shows up years later, in all the bad decisions nobody was willing to interrupt.

## Practical Application

- Thank someone who once stopped you from a decision you would have regretted, and name what they prevented.
- Make it clear to your team that stepping into the road is welcome.
- When someone stops you, let the thanks change what you do next.

## Takeaways

- David thanked God and thanked the person, and he named what was prevented.
- Resenting the person who stopped you teaches everyone else to stay quiet.

## Closing Thought

David blessed the one who stood in his road, and then he obeyed her voice.
