## Scripture

**Deuteronomy 24:14–15 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The instruction is practical and the reason sits right inside it. Pay the worker the same day, before the sun sets, for he is poor and counts on it. The timing matters because of what the delay does to the person waiting on it.

Nothing here involves fraud. The wage is not being stolen. It is being held, and holding it is named as oppression because of the gap between what the delay costs the employer and what it costs the worker.

Leaders sit on things constantly. The approval that takes three weeks. The reimbursement that waits for the next cycle. The decision someone needs before they can plan their month. None of it feels like oppression from the leader's chair, because the leader is not the one counting on it.

Stewardship of people includes the speed of your own hand. A leader who is slow with what others depend on is spending something that belongs to them, usually without ever learning the exchange rate.

Lest he cry against you to the LORD, and you be guilty of sin moves the matter outside the employer's own judgment. The worker's dependence sets the deadline, not the employer's convenience.

## Practical Application

- Find what someone is waiting on from you right now and clear it today.
- Ask whether your approval cycles are built around your convenience or the needs of the people inside them.
- Pay someone early once, and watch what it does for them.

## Takeaways

- Holding what is owed is treated as oppression, even when nothing has been stolen.
- A leader who is slow with what others depend on is spending something that belongs to them.

## Closing Thought

The deadline was set by what the worker was counting on.
