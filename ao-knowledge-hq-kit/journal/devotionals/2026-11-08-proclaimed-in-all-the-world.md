## Scripture

**Romans 1:8 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders are quick to celebrate wins they had a hand in. The team that hit the number on their watch, the hire they made that worked out. Good work happening somewhere else, in a division they do not run or a company down the street, tends to get a nod at most, and sometimes a flicker of envy.

Paul's first move in this letter is thanks. First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you. The reason is a report. Their faith is proclaimed in all the world, and he is thankful for it the way he would be for people he had led himself.

That is harder than it sounds. Thankfulness for your own people can be partly pride wearing better clothes. Thankfulness for people you did not build has no credit attached to it. It only works if a leader actually cares more about the good being done than about who gets to claim it.

Notice too who is included. For all of you. Not the best of them or the ones he already knew. The whole group gets the thanks, which is a habit worth borrowing for any leader who tends to thank the same three standouts every time.

Leaders who can be thankful for good work outside their own walls become safer people to be around. Peers stop guarding information from them and start sharing what is working. That kind of generosity tends to come back around, though that is not the reason to practice it.

## Practical Application

- Name someone outside your team doing good work and thank God for them by name.
- Tell that leader directly what you have heard about their work.
- When you thank your own team, include everyone, not only the usual standouts.

## Takeaways

- Thankfulness for work you did not build carries no credit, which is what makes it honest.
- Thanks that includes everyone keeps the quiet contributors from disappearing.

## Closing Thought

Paul's first word to these people was thanks for faith he had only heard about.
