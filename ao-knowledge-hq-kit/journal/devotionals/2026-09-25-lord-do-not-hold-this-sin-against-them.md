## Scripture

**Acts 7:59–60 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Stephen's final words follow the same shape as the words spoken from the cross, and that's not a coincidence worth glossing over. He's dying by execution, actively being stoned as he speaks, and his last recorded act is asking that the sin being committed against him right now not be counted against the people doing it.

This is imitation in the most literal sense, someone who witnessed or knew of Christ's example and, at the actual moment it would cost him everything to apply it, applied it anyway. It's one thing to admire a costly example from a safe distance. It's another to reach for it in the specific moment your own life is ending because of the very people you're asking to be forgiven.

Falling to his knees while crying this out physically enacts the posture of the request itself, a man lowering himself even further in the act of extending mercy to people who had shown him none. There's no dignity being reclaimed here, no final assertion of power over the situation. The posture matches the content of what's being said.

A leader who has genuinely internalized the mercy this month has described, not just agreed with it intellectually but actually absorbed it as a way of responding, should be able to locate it under pressure, not just in comfortable moments when extending grace costs nothing. Stephen's example is extreme, but the underlying test isn't different in kind from smaller versions a leader faces regularly, whether the mercy shows up only when it's easy or whether it's actually there when the cost is real.

He fell asleep is a striking way to describe death in this context, language usually reserved for something peaceful rather than violent. Whatever peace Stephen had in that moment seems inseparable from the mercy he'd just extended rather than despite it.

## Practical Application

- Notice whether your own capacity for mercy only shows up when it's comfortable to extend.
- Identify a costly example someone else has modeled for you, and consider applying it in a harder moment than you have so far.
- Extend grace this week in a situation where it will cost you something real, rather than where it's convenient.

## Takeaways

- Stephen applies the example he'd witnessed at the exact moment it would cost him everything, not from a safe distance.
- Genuine mercy shows up under real pressure. Mercy that only appears when it's comfortable hasn't actually been tested yet.

## Closing Thought

He asked for mercy on their behalf while they were still actively taking his life.
