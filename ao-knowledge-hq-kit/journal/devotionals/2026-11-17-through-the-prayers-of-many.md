## Scripture

**2 Corinthians 1:8–11 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Most leaders tell their people about a crisis after it is over, if at all. The cash crunch, the lawsuit, the month they were not sure the company would survive. By the time anyone hears about it, it is a story with a happy ending, told in a way that keeps the leader looking steady.

Paul tells it while it still stings. We were so utterly burdened beyond our strength that we despaired of life itself. He does not round it off. He wants these people to know exactly how bad it was.

He also tells them what it did to him. That was to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead. The affliction stripped away the option of self-reliance. Leaders rarely give up self-reliance voluntarily. It usually has to be taken from them.

Then he asks for help. You also must help us by prayer, so that many will give thanks on our behalf. The thankfulness spreads in proportion to how many people were let in. People who were never told could not pray, and people who did not pray have nothing to give thanks for when the rescue comes.

Hiding every crisis protects a leader's image and costs their people something. They lose the chance to carry part of the load, and they lose the thanks that comes from seeing a prayer answered. Letting people in costs some appearance of strength. It gives the whole team a share in the deliverance.

## Practical Application

- Tell someone you trust about a burden you are carrying while it is still heavy.
- Ask specific people to pray for something specific.
- When the answer comes, go back and give thanks with the people who prayed.

## Takeaways

- Paul told people how bad it was so they could help carry it.
- Thankfulness grows with the number of people who were let in.

## Closing Thought

Many gave thanks because many had been told.
