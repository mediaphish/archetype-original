## Scripture

**Exodus 34:6–7 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Moses asked to see God's glory, and what he got instead was God speaking his own name aloud. Not a vision, a description. Merciful and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in steadfast love. Then, in the same sentence, without a pause to soften it, will by no means clear the guilty. God does not offer the flattering half of his character and leave the rest for later. He states both at once, as if separating them would be the actual distortion.

A leader reads a sentence like that and wants to file it under contradiction. Mercy belongs in one column, justice in the other, and a leader picks a side depending on the day and the person in front of them. This passage refuses the filing system. The same God who forgives iniquity and transgression and sin is the God who will not clear the guilty. Nothing here is walked back to make room for the other half.

What that means for a leader is specific and uncomfortable. Holding a standard does not earn you an exemption from mercy later, and extending mercy does not require you to first lower the standard to make room for it. They are not traded against each other like a budget with a fixed total. A leader who thinks they have to choose has misread both what mercy is and what a standard is for.

This is also, without question, the hardest verse in the whole month to actually live out, because it asks for something that looks like two different people occupying one leader at the same time. Firm enough that the guilty are still named as guilty. Merciful enough that grace is not rationed out only to the innocent. Most leaders default to one and call it their strength, when the passage is asking for both, held without collapsing into either.

Every leader in the days ahead this month, the ones who get this right and the ones who don't, are being measured against this proclamation first. It is not a footnote to the theme. It is the definition of it.

## Practical Application

- Write down one place where you've quietly decided mercy means the standard has to bend. Test whether that's actually true.
- Hold a standard and extend grace in the same conversation this week, on purpose, without treating them as a trade.
- Notice anywhere you've mistaken mercy for weakness, and correct the record with yourself first.

## Takeaways

- Mercy and an unmoved standard are stated together in the same breath, not traded against each other.
- A leader who treats mercy and justice as opposites has misunderstood what both of them are for.

## Closing Thought

Nothing in this proclamation was softened to make room for the rest of it.
