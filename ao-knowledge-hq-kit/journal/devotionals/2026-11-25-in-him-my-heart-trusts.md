## Scripture

**Psalm 28:7 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders are usually the shield for someone else. They absorb the angry client, stand between the team and the board, take the call nobody else wants. Over time they can forget that they need protecting too, and they start to treat being helped as a sign that they have failed.

David says it plainly. The LORD is my strength and my shield. Then the order of what follows. In him my heart trusts, and I am helped. The trust comes first, then the help. He is not describing someone who held everything up alone and then gave thanks for his own endurance.

I am helped is a simple sentence, and many leaders find it hard to say. It admits that the strength came from somewhere else. It is also true of every leader who has ever made it through something they could not have carried alone.

My heart exults, and with my song I give thanks to him. The joy and the thanks come at the end, after the trust and the help. Thankfulness here is not a technique for getting through. It is what spills out of someone who trusted, was helped and knows exactly who did the helping.

A leader who can say I am helped stops pretending to be the only shield in the room. That honesty gives their people room to ask for help without shame. Thankfulness that names God as strength and shield makes it safe for everyone else to need one.

## Practical Application

- Name one place this year where you were helped, and thank God for it specifically.
- Tell someone who assumes you did it alone that you were helped.
- Make it easier for someone on your team to ask for help.

## Takeaways

- Trust comes first, then help, then thanks.
- A leader who can say I am helped makes it safe for others to say it too.

## Closing Thought

Leaders shield a lot of people, and David still knew who his shield was.
