## Scripture

**Psalm 139:23–24 (ESV)**

## Reflection

This prayer closes a psalm about being fully known, every word, every thought, every place, already seen before it happens. David does not end there with comfort alone. He turns the fact of being fully known into an invitation: search me, know my heart, see if there is anything grievous in me. He asks to be examined on purpose.

This is a different posture than the accountability this week has mostly described from the outside, tending to people, watching for their needs, being an example rather than a tyrant. This closing day turns the accountability inward. A leader who is genuinely accountable to those they lead has to first be willing to be searched themselves, not just to search the situations of the people around them.

Leaders can become skilled at examining everyone else's performance, motives, and blind spots while quietly exempting their own from the same scrutiny. It is a natural asymmetry. The leader holds the position that does the evaluating. David refuses that asymmetry for himself. He does not wait for someone else to point out a grievous way in him. He asks God to find it first.

Servant leadership does not treat self-examination as optional once accountability to others is being taken seriously. A leader who tends carefully to the people they lead while never inviting the same searching scrutiny into their own heart has only done half of what this week has described. Accountability to those you lead is completed by accountability to what is actually in you, invited rather than avoided.

## Practical Application

- Pray this prayer honestly this week, and mean the invitation, not just the words.
- Ask someone close to you what grievous way, if any, they see in your leadership.
- Receive the answer without defending yourself first.

## Takeaways

- David does not wait to be examined; he asks for it, turning the fact of being known into an invitation to be searched.
- A leader who tends to others while never inviting the same scrutiny into their own heart has only done half of what accountability requires.

## Closing Thought

Accountability to those you lead is completed by accountability to what is actually in you, invited rather than avoided.
