## Scripture

**1 Thessalonians 1:2–3 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Most recognition goes to output. The launch, the closed deal, the quarter that came in over plan. The person who kept a struggling account alive for two years without a win rarely makes the slide, because nothing they did fits in a single number.

The thanks here is for something underneath the output. Your work of faith and labor of love and steadfastness of hope. Work, labor, steadfastness. Each one is effort that costs something, and each one is named by what was driving it.

Steadfastness of hope is the one leaders most often miss. It describes people who kept going when nothing visible was rewarding them for it. Endurance rarely announces itself, so a leader has to go looking for it in order to be thankful for it.

The other detail is when the remembering happens. Constantly mentioning you in our prayers, remembering before our God and Father. These people are not in the room. They are remembered anyway, in detail, with thanks. What a leader says about people when they are absent tells the truth about what that leader actually values.

Thankfulness for effort does not lower the bar on results. It tells people that what they put in is seen, even when the result is slow to come. People who know their endurance is noticed tend to endure longer, and that is often the difference between a team that quits early and one that finally gets the win.

## Practical Application

- Thank someone for how long they have stuck with something hard, not for the outcome.
- Notice how you talk about your people when they are not in the room.
- Look for the steady contributor whose work never fits on a slide.

## Takeaways

- Effort and endurance deserve thanks before the results arrive.
- What a leader says about people in their absence shows what that leader values.

## Closing Thought

Work, labor and steadfastness were all remembered, and none of them showed up on a scoreboard.
