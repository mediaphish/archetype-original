## Scripture

**1 Samuel 12:23 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Samuel says this at the exact moment his formal authority ends. Israel has demanded a king, Saul has been anointed, and Samuel's decades of leading the nation are effectively over. He has every reason to step back entirely. Instead he makes a commitment that outlasts his title: he will keep praying for the people and keep instructing them, whether or not his position requires it anymore.

This reframes what accountability to those you lead actually depends on. It is easy to assume accountability is a function of authority, that a leader is accountable to people because the leader has power over them, and once the power ends, so does the accountability. Samuel treats it the other way. His accountability to the people was never really about the title. It was about the people, and the people have not gone anywhere just because his role has changed.

Leaders experience versions of this constantly on a smaller scale: a project handed to someone else, a team restructured, a role that no longer includes people you once led directly. The formal accountability ends cleanly. Whether a leader still cares enough to pray, to instruct, to stay engaged with what happens to those people, is a different question entirely, and it reveals whether the accountability was ever really about the people or mostly about the position.

Servant leadership treats accountability to those you lead as something that does not expire when the org chart changes. Samuel could have walked away with his reputation intact. No one would have faulted him. He chose instead to keep serving the people he was no longer required to serve, because the accountability he felt toward them had never been contingent on his title in the first place.

## Practical Application

- Think of someone you once led directly whom you no longer have formal responsibility for.
- Identify one way you could still support or check in on them this month.
- Do it without needing the authority to make it your job.

## Takeaways

- Samuel's accountability to the people was never really about his title; it did not end when his role did.
- Whether a leader stays engaged after formal authority ends reveals whether the accountability was ever about the people or mostly about the position.

## Closing Thought

Real accountability to those you lead does not expire the moment your authority does.
