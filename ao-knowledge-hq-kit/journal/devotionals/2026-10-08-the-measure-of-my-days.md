## Scripture

**Psalm 39:4–5 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders plan in quarters and years, and quietly assume there will be more of both. Calendars fill with things that can always be moved later. The assumption underneath all of it is that time is the one resource that keeps arriving on its own.

The prayer here asks for something most people work hard to avoid. Make me know my end. Let me know how fleeting I am. That is a request to feel the limit rather than forget it, which is the opposite of how most leaders handle a calendar.

A few handbreadths is a small measurement, roughly the width of a hand. Set against that, the pace of a leader's ambition looks different. Not less valuable, just bounded. Every project, every build, every five-year plan sits inside a life shorter than the plan assumes.

Stewardship of time starts with accepting that the supply is fixed and the total is unknown. A leader cannot buy more of it, borrow it from next year, or make it up later. What gets spent on a meeting that did not need to happen is gone in a way money never is, because money can be earned back.

Knowing the measure is not meant to paralyze anyone. It orders things. A leader who has honestly asked how fleeting I am tends to protect the few things that matter and stop pretending the rest can be handled someday.

## Practical Application

- Look back at your last month of calendar and name what you would not spend that time on again.
- Take one thing you keep postponing that genuinely matters and give it a date.
- Say no to one request that only fits if something important gets pushed.

## Takeaways

- Time is the one entrusted resource a leader cannot earn back.
- Asking to feel the limit is what reorders priorities, and forgetting it is what fills a calendar with things that never mattered.

## Closing Thought

Money can be earned back. The afternoon cannot.
