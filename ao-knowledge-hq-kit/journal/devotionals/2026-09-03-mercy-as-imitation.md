## Scripture

**Luke 6:35–36 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The command to be merciful arrives with a comparison already attached to it. Be merciful, even as your Father is merciful, and the sentence right before it has already told you what that Father's mercy actually looks like in practice. Kind to the ungrateful. Kind to the evil. Not kind to the people who will thank him for it, or the people who deserve it by any ordinary measure of deserving.

That comparison sets a bar most leaders never quite reach, because most leadership mercy is conditional without anyone admitting it's conditional. Patience for the team member who's clearly trying. Grace for the client who apologizes well. That kind of mercy costs almost nothing, because it's extended to people who make it easy to extend. The Father's mercy in this passage is aimed in the opposite direction, at people who won't return the favor and may never even notice it was given.

That is the actual imitation being asked for here, not general kindness but kindness with the direction reversed. A leader who only shows mercy to people who've earned goodwill isn't imitating anything. They're transacting. The instruction is to be merciful the way the Father is merciful, toward the person least likely to say thank you, which means the leader has to let go of needing the thank you at all.

This is where a lot of leaders quietly stall out. They can manage generosity when it's convenient or when it will be noticed. What this passage is describing is a fixed posture that doesn't check first whether mercy will be appreciated before deciding to extend it. The mercy comes first. Whatever comes back afterward, gratitude or nothing at all, doesn't change the decision that was already made.

Held next to what Exodus 34 said yesterday about God's own character, this passage is the same claim turned into an instruction. Not just observe that God is merciful to the ungrateful. Be that way yourself.

## Practical Application

- Name the person on your team least likely to thank you for patience, and extend it to them this week anyway.
- Separate the decision to be merciful from any expectation of how it will be received.
- Ask honestly whether your mercy so far has mostly gone to people who made it easy.

## Takeaways

- Mercy modeled after the Father is aimed at people unlikely to return it, not people who make it easy.
- Needing gratitude in return turns mercy into a transaction instead of an imitation.

## Closing Thought

The mercy asked for here doesn't wait to see if it will be appreciated first.
