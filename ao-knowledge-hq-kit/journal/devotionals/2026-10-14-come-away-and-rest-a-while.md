## Scripture

**Mark 6:30–31 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The team comes back from real work with plenty to report. The obvious next move is to keep going. Demand is high, people are coming and going, and momentum is finally on their side.

The instruction runs the other way. Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while. Not once the crowd thins out. Not after the work is finished. In the middle of it, while the need is still standing there in plain view.

Most leaders would not have called for that. Rest tends to get granted when it has been earned or when somebody breaks down. Offering it while the work is going well feels like leaving results on the table, and the leader is usually the one who would have collected those results.

Stewardship covers other people's time too, including the parts of it a leader never schedules. A team with no leisure even to eat is being spent faster than it is being restored. Leaders miss this because they watch output, and output is the last thing to fall.

Come away by yourselves also makes the rest specific. A place, a group, a stated permission to stop. Vague encouragement to take care of yourself is not the same thing as a leader naming the time, protecting it, and going along.

## Practical Application

- Look at the pace of one person you lead and ask whether they are being spent faster than they are restored.
- Name a specific time for rest instead of encouraging it in general.
- Take that rest with them rather than sending them off while you keep working.

## Takeaways

- Rest offered in the middle of good work is a different decision than rest granted after a collapse.
- Output is the last thing to fall, so it cannot be the signal a leader waits for.

## Closing Thought

The instruction came while the crowd was still arriving.
