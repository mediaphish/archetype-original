## Scripture

**Luke 10:31, 33–35 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Compassion in this passage isn't the end of the sentence. It's the first clause of a much longer one. The Samaritan had compassion, and then he went to him, bound the wounds, provided the transport, paid for the care, and left an open-ended promise to cover whatever more it cost. Feeling something for the wounded man was the beginning of the response, not the whole of it.

That distinction matters because compassion alone is available to everyone, including the priest who passed by on the other side just before this. It's entirely possible to feel something and keep walking. What separates the Samaritan isn't that he felt more than the priest did. It's that his compassion actually turned into oil, wine, transportation, and money, in that order, without stopping at the feeling.

The two denarii and the promise to cover whatever more it costs are worth sitting with specifically. This wasn't a single act completed and closed. It was an open financial commitment made to a stranger, with no guarantee the man would ever be able to repay it or even remember who helped him. Mercy extended this far has genuinely left the giver exposed.

A leader's mercy is measured the same way. Sympathy costs nothing and can be offered freely without any real exposure. Actually binding the wounds, actually covering the cost, actually leaving an open account for whatever more it takes, requires a leader to be willing to absorb an amount they can't fully predict in advance.

The Samaritan didn't know the wounded man, owed him nothing, and had every reasonable excuse the priest apparently found sufficient. He went to him anyway, and the going is the part of mercy that separates the story that gets told from the ones that don't.

## Practical Application

- Identify a place where you've felt compassion for someone but stopped short of actually acting on it.
- Consider what an open-ended commitment, rather than a single closed gesture, would look like in a current situation.
- Go to someone this week rather than simply feeling something for them from a distance.

## Takeaways

- Compassion that stops at the feeling is available to anyone. Compassion that turns into action is what the passage actually commends.
- An open-ended commitment, willing to cover whatever more it costs, is a further step than a single closed act of kindness.

## Closing Thought

He didn't just feel something for the man in the road. He went to him.
