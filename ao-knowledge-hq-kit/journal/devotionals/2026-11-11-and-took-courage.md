## Scripture

**Acts 28:14–15 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders who have been through a brutal stretch often arrive at the next assignment worn down. The crisis is over, but the cost has not been paid off yet. What they need in that moment is rarely a strategy. It is usually a person.

Paul comes to Rome at the end of a voyage that nearly killed everyone on board. The brothers there heard he was coming and came as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns to meet him. They did not wait for him to arrive. They went out to where he was.

On seeing them, Paul thanked God and took courage. The order matters. Seeing them came first, thanks came second and courage came third. The courage did not come from a plan or a speech. It came through thankfulness for people who showed up.

Leaders tend to think of courage as something they supply to others. Here it flows the other direction. The man who spent the storm telling everyone else to take heart is now the one who needs it, and the people who give it to him are people whose names the text never records.

Thankfulness for people opens a leader up to receive from them. Leaders who only hand out encouragement and never let anyone give it back end up running on empty. Sometimes the most important thing a team can do is walk out to meet the leader, and the most important thing the leader can do is let them.

## Practical Application

- Name the people who showed up for you during your hardest stretch, and thank God for them.
- Go out to meet someone on your team who is coming off a hard season.
- Let someone encourage you without deflecting it.

## Takeaways

- Courage often reaches a leader through people, by way of thankfulness.
- The people who walk out to meet you are worth more than any plan.

## Closing Thought

He saw them, he thanked God and then he took courage.
