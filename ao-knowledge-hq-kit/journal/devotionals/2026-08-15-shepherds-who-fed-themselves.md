## Scripture

**Ezekiel 34:2–4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The charge against these shepherds is not that they mismanaged the flock through incompetence. It is that they inverted the purpose of the role entirely. They took the position meant to provide for the sheep and used it to provide for themselves. The wool, the meat, the comfort of the position, all of it flowed inward instead of outward.

This week turns from accountability for what a leader was given to accountability for who a leader was given to lead. It is a harder kind of accountability, because it is measured in people rather than resources, and people do not show up cleanly in an account the way money or time does. Verse four names five specific failures: the weak not strengthened, the sick not healed, the injured not bound up, the strayed not brought back, the lost not sought. Each one describes a person the shepherds could see and chose not to tend.

Leaders rarely announce, even to themselves, that they are feeding on the position rather than feeding the people in it. The shift is usually smaller and slower: attention drifts toward what the role provides before it drifts toward who the role serves. A leader can maintain every outward function of the job while the actual accountability, tending to the people entrusted to them, quietly stops happening.

Servant leadership measures itself by the specific list in verse four, not by the general fact of holding the position. Accountability to those you lead is not satisfied by occupying the role faithfully. It is satisfied by the weak being strengthened, the injured being bound up, the lost being sought. A shepherd who is well fed while the sheep go unattended has failed at the one thing the role was for.

## Practical Application

- Name someone on your team who currently fits one of the five categories in verse four: weak, sick, injured, strayed, or lost.
- Identify what tending them would actually require of you this week.
- Do that thing, not just the general work of leading well.

## Takeaways

- The shepherds' failure was not incompetence. It was using the role to provide for themselves instead of the people in it.
- Accountability to those you lead is not satisfied by holding the position faithfully. It is satisfied by specific people being specifically tended.

## Closing Thought

A shepherd who is well fed while the sheep go unattended has failed at the one thing the role was for.
