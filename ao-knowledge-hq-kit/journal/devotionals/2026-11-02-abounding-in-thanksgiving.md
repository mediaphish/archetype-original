## Scripture

**Colossians 2:6–7 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Some leaders say thank you the way they sign an email. It is correct, brief and finished before anyone has felt it. Nothing is wrong with it, and nothing much comes from it either.

The picture here is of something growing. Roots going down, a structure going up, a foundation getting firm, and at the end of the sentence, thanksgiving that abounds. Abounding is the language of overflow. It describes more thankfulness than the moment strictly required.

Overflow does not come from effort at the surface. It comes from what is underneath. A leader rooted in something deeper than the current role has more to draw from when the role gets hard, and the thanks keeps coming in seasons when a shallower leader would run dry. Without the roots, gratitude becomes something a leader manages rather than something that spills over.

Then there is the phrase just as you were taught. Someone showed these people what it looked like to walk this way. The people under any leader are learning the same way, mostly by watching. A team learns whether gratitude is real or procedural from the person at the front of the room.

Leadership that abounds in thanksgiving is not louder. It is deeper, and the depth shows up as more than enough.

## Practical Application

- Thank someone today in a way that takes longer than the email would.
- Ask what your thankfulness is rooted in, and whether it would survive a hard season.
- Notice what your team is learning about gratitude by watching you.

## Takeaways

- Abounding thanksgiving is overflow, and overflow comes from roots rather than effort.
- Teams learn whether gratitude is real by watching the leader, not by hearing about it.

## Closing Thought

Thin roots produce polite thanks, and deep ones produce more than the moment asked for.
