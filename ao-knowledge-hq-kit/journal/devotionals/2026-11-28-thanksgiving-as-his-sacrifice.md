### November Overview

November asked what it looks like for a leader to be thankful on purpose. October spent a month on stewardship, tending what had been entrusted. Thankfulness is what a steward feels on noticing how much of it was given in the first place.

We started with thankfulness as a practice. Gates entered with thanksgiving before anyone knew how the day would go. Thanks that overflows from roots rather than effort. Steadfast love declared in the morning and faithfulness at night. One man out of ten who turned around and came back. Promises kept as a way of rendering thanks. An answer to prayer that Daniel shared with the three who asked with him. And David giving thankfulness a role and a set of names.

Then thankfulness for people. Faith reported from a city Paul had not yet visited. Thanks for a difficult church before a long correction. Work, labor and steadfastness noticed rather than results. Courage that arrived when friends came out to meet a prisoner. Abigail stopping David from a decision he would have regretted. The joy of hearing that people you love are walking in the truth. And two names read out with what they risked.

Then thankfulness that costs something. Every source of income failing, followed by yet I will rejoice. Thanks spoken while still in pain. A crisis shared so that many could pray and many could give thanks. Mourning turned to dancing and a song that was not supposed to stop. An inheritance kept where trouble cannot reach it. A rejected stone that became the cornerstone. And thanksgiving spoken around the throne, right between wisdom and honor.

We finished with what thankfulness produces. Gladness that follows a careful record. Good gifts received without guilt. An invitation that begins with let us. A heart that trusts, is helped and gives thanks. Generosity that knows where it came from. Steadiness built on a victory already won. And a life ordered by thanks offered as a sacrifice.

Advent comes next, the season of waiting for the coming of Christ. It always begins on the fourth Sunday before Christmas, and that Sunday often lands before November is over, so this month closes a few days early and Advent begins with the next post. Thankfulness is good preparation for it. A leader who has spent a month counting what was already given is ready to wait well for what is still coming.

## Scripture

**Psalm 50:23 (ESV)**

## Reflection

A sacrifice is something given up. It costs the person who brings it. Most leaders think of thankfulness as the opposite, a pleasant response that costs nothing and comes easily when things go well.

God says something different here. The one who offers thanksgiving as his sacrifice glorifies me. Thanksgiving is the offering. It is placed on the altar the way an animal or a harvest would have been, which means it is meant to cost something and meant to be given on purpose.

Thanks offered only when it is easy is not much of a sacrifice. Thanks offered when the numbers are down and the answer has not come yet costs the person something real. That is the kind God says glorifies him.

Then a second line. To one who orders his way rightly I will show the salvation of God. Offering thanks and ordering a way rightly sit in the same verse. Thankfulness is not a feeling kept separate from how a leader actually lives. It shapes the order of the days, the decisions and the direction.

A month of giving thanks is meant to become a way of ordering a life, not an exercise that ends with the calendar. A leader who keeps offering thanksgiving after the holiday has passed will find it ordering their priorities, their words and the way they treat people long after November is over.

## Practical Application

- Offer thanks for something that costs you to be thankful for.
- Choose one practice from this month on thankfulness to keep going into December.
- Let thankfulness shape one decision you are about to make.

## Takeaways

- Thanksgiving offered as a sacrifice is meant to cost something.
- Offering thanks and ordering a way rightly belong in the same verse.

## Closing Thought

The offering God asked for was thanks, given on purpose.
