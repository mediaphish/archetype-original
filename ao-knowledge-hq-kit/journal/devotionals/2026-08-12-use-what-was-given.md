## Scripture

**Romans 12:6–8 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul does not describe a single gift distributed evenly. He describes different gifts, given by grace, each with its own manner of use. The list is specific on purpose: teaching is taught, leading is led with zeal, mercy is shown with cheerfulness. Accountability here is not about matching someone else's gift. It is about actually using the one you were given, in the way suited to it.

This is a different kind of accountability than the ones this week has covered so far. It is not about guarding a resource or refusing an unearned privilege. It is about the failure of leaving a genuine gift unused, whether from insecurity, distraction, or simply defaulting to someone else's strengths instead of developing your own.

Leaders are especially prone to this failure with the gift of leading itself. Verse eight names it plainly: the one who leads, with zeal. Not leading tentatively while apologizing for the authority it requires. Not leading by borrowing someone else's style because it looks more comfortable. Leading, as the gift it is, with the energy the gift calls for.

Servant leadership takes accountability for the gift as seriously as it takes accountability for the resource or the title. A leader who has real capacity to lead and exercises it half-heartedly is not being humble. They are letting something they were given by grace go unused, and that is its own form of unfaithfulness.

## Practical Application

- Name your actual gift for leading, teaching, serving, or encouraging, not the gift you wish you had.
- Identify one place you have been using it half-heartedly or borrowing someone else's approach instead.
- Use it fully, in its own manner, this week.

## Takeaways

- Accountability for a gift means actually using it in the way suited to it, not matching someone else's gift.
- A leader with real capacity who exercises it half-heartedly is not being humble; they are letting a gift go unused.

## Closing Thought

A gift left unused is still a form of unfaithfulness, even when it looks like humility.
