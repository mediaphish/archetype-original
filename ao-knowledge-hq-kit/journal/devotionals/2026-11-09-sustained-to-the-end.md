## Scripture

**1 Corinthians 1:4–9 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Every leader eventually inherits a group that is hard to lead. Talented, opinionated, divided and a little proud of it. The temptation is to open every conversation with the problem, because the problem is what keeps the leader up at night.

The letter to Corinth spends chapter after chapter correcting the people it is written to. It opens with thanks. I give thanks to my God always for you because of the grace of God that was given you in Christ Jesus. The correction is coming, and the thanks comes first anyway.

The ground of the thanks is worth noticing. It is not their behavior. It is what was given to them. In every way you were enriched, not lacking in any gift. Paul is thankful for what God put in these people, which is a different thing from being thankful for how they are using it.

Then the confidence moves forward. He will sustain you to the end, guiltless. God is faithful. The thankfulness does not depend on the group getting its act together. It rests on someone else's faithfulness, which means a leader can be honest about the problems without giving up on the people.

Leaders who open with real thanks earn a hearing for the hard part. Teams can tell the difference between a compliment used to soften a blow and a leader who genuinely sees what is in them. The first is a technique. The second is the reason correction from that leader is worth listening to.

## Practical Application

- Before your next hard conversation, write down what is genuinely good in the person or group.
- Thank them for what they have been given, not only for what they have done with it.
- Let your confidence in your people rest on more than their latest performance.

## Takeaways

- Thankfulness for what people have been given can sit right beside honest correction.
- A leader who sees what is in people earns a hearing for the hard part.

## Closing Thought

The letter that corrects them most begins by thanking God for them.
