## Scripture

**Romans 16:3–4 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Ask a leader who made their success possible and most will answer in general terms. A great team, a supportive family, good mentors. It sounds humble. It also lets every specific person who carried the load disappear into a phrase.

Paul does not speak in categories here. He gives names. Prisca and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their necks for my life. Two people, one plain description of what they did and an honest admission that he is alive because of them.

Then the thanks widens. Not only I give thanks but all the churches of the Gentiles give thanks as well. What these two did for one man reached people they would never meet. Leaders forget this. The person who protected the leader also protected everything the leader went on to build.

Fellow workers is the title Paul gives them. Not helpers or supporters. He places them alongside himself, in writing, where everyone who heard the letter would know it.

Public thankfulness that names people changes a culture. It tells everyone listening what kind of contribution counts, and it gives the people named something they will remember for years. Generic thanks costs nothing and is forgotten by lunch. Specific thanks, said where others can hear it, gets repeated.

## Practical Application

- Replace one general thank you with the name of a specific person and what they did.
- Thank someone publicly for a risk they took on your behalf.
- Call the people who carry the load alongside you fellow workers, and mean it.

## Takeaways

- Thankfulness in categories lets the specific people who carried the load disappear.
- What one person did for a leader often reaches people they will never meet.

## Closing Thought

Paul could have thanked everyone in general, and instead he named two.
