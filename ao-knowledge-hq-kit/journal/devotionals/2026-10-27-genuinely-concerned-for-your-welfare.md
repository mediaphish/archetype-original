## Scripture

**Philippians 2:19–21 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Paul is sending his most useful person away, and the reason given is other people's welfare rather than his own convenience. That decision costs him something immediately and he makes it anyway.

For I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare. The compliment is specific. Not the most talented. Not the most impressive in a room. The one who actually cares what happens to people he is not currently standing in front of.

Then the hard line. They all seek their own interests. Paul is describing the people around him, and the scarcity he names is the ordinary condition of most teams. Competence is common. Genuine concern for somebody else's welfare is not.

Stewardship of people shows up in who a leader develops and where they send them. Keeping your best person close because they make your own work easier is defensible right up to the point where it becomes the only reason.

The letter also tells the Philippians what they are receiving, which is its own act of care. A leader who sends someone good, and says plainly why they are good, hands over a reputation along with a person.

## Practical Application

- Name the person on your team who is genuinely concerned for others, and tell them you see it.
- Consider who you are keeping close because it benefits you rather than them.
- When you send someone to help another team, say plainly what makes them worth having.

## Takeaways

- Competence is common and genuine concern for others is not, which makes the second one worth protecting.
- Who a leader sends, and where, says more than who they keep.

## Closing Thought

The rarest thing he could say about anyone was that he actually cared.
