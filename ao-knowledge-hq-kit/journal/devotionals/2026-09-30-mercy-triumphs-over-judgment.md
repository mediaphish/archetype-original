### September Overview

September addressed grace and mercy, not as something a leader receives and moves past, but as something a leader extends to the people under them, the same way August asked what it means to give an honest account.

We began with the standard itself. What the Lord requires. Merciful and just at once. Mercy as imitation of a Father kind to the ungrateful. The measure of forgiveness fixed to what was already received. Every leader in these early days, Joseph, Jonah, the one restoring another gently, was measured against a mercy that never traded away the standard to make room for itself.

We moved into what mercy looks like extended specifically. A tender heart kept alive rather than hardened by experience. Kindness rooted in an old commitment, not present performance. A father running to meet a son still a long way off. The glory of overlooking an offense. A debt charged to someone else's account. A Samaritan whose compassion became oil, wine, and an open promise to cover whatever more it cost.

We spent a week on the hardest pairing the month offered, mercy that still corrects. Discipline named as evidence of love, not its absence. Reproof aimed at people still claimed, not written off. Patience broad enough to cover the full range from gentle correction to direct rebuke. The wanderer pursued before the drift became something worse. Strength redirected toward carrying the vulnerable rather than driving them.

We closed with the enemy, the hardest test the whole arc could pose. Love commanded toward the very people working against you. A brother who ran to meet the one who wronged him before an apology was ever offered. Forgiveness spoken into the middle of an execution, and again from a man on his knees being stoned. An enemy fed rather than merely spared. Small failures covered continuously rather than kept on a ledger. And the reciprocity underneath all of it: the mercy shown and the mercy eventually received turning out to be the same thing.

October will turn toward stewardship, what a leader does with everything that has been entrusted to them, fitting for the month that carries Bart's birthday. That arc is still ahead.

## Scripture

**James 2:13 (ESV)**

## Reflection

James states this as a fixed rule, not a suggestion. Judgment without mercy is specifically reserved for the one who showed no mercy. The severity a leader can expect is directly tied to the severity they were willing to extend, which makes this less a warning about God's character in the abstract and more a direct statement about consequence tied to practice.

Mercy triumphs over judgment closes the verse with something closer to a promise than a threat. Whatever accounting a leader might otherwise face, mercy shown genuinely changes the outcome. This isn't mercy earning a leader an exemption from having done wrong. It's mercy actually altering what judgment produces when it arrives.

This verse functions as the sharpest possible summary of everything the month has walked through. The leader who withheld mercy from a team member's failure, who refused to forgive a small debt after being forgiven a large one, who judged a shared failure in someone else while presuming on their own kindness, is the leader this verse names directly. The consequence isn't abstract. It's proportional to what was refused.

Triumphs is a strong word to end on, not merely mitigates or softens. Mercy doesn't just take the edge off judgment. It overcomes it entirely for the leader who has genuinely practiced it. That's a considerably higher stake than most leaders assign to their daily choices about whether to extend grace or withhold it in some minor interaction that seems too small to matter.

Every day this month has been building toward this exact claim, that what a leader extends is not incidental to what they can expect, but the actual determining factor. James states it as flatly as it can be stated. The leader closing out September has spent thirty days practicing the very thing this verse says will triumph.

## Practical Application

- Name one place this month where you've genuinely practiced mercy rather than just believed in it.
- Identify anywhere you're still withholding mercy that this verse would call you to reconsider directly.
- Carry the practice of mercy into October as the posture stewardship will be built on.

## Takeaways

- The judgment a leader can expect is tied directly to the mercy they were willing to extend, not abstractly related to it.
- Mercy is described as triumphing over judgment entirely, not merely softening it.

## Closing Thought

What was extended and what will ultimately triumph turn out to be the same thing.
