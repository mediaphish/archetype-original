## Scripture

**Proverbs 6:6–8 (ESV)**

## Reflection

The ant has no chief, no officer and no ruler, and the work still gets done in the right order at the right time. That detail is pointed directly at people who need to be told.

Most leaders have been the officer in someone else's life. They have also been the one who only moved when a deadline or a boss required it. The proverb quietly asks which of those a leader actually is when nobody is checking.

Preparing bread in summer and gathering food in harvest is not complicated work. It is ordinary work, done at the right time, over and over, without supervision. Stewardship of time looks far more like that than like heroics under deadline pressure.

There is a second thing here for anyone responsible for others. A team that works only when watched has usually been trained to. Leaders who supervise every step produce people who wait for the next step to be assigned. The aim is people who gather in summer because they understand what winter does, not because someone is standing over them.

Go to the ant, O sluggard is blunt on purpose. The comparison is meant to sting, and it stings most for the leader who has mistaken being busy under pressure for having been diligent all along.

## Practical Application

- Notice what you only do when someone is expecting it, and do one of those things before anyone asks.
- Ask honestly whether your team needs supervision or has been trained to need it.
- Pick one winter you can already see coming and start gathering for it.

## Takeaways

- Diligence without supervision is the standard the proverb sets, for a leader and for a team.
- People who work only when watched have usually been led that way.

## Closing Thought

No chief, no officer, no ruler, and the work still got done on time.
