## Scripture

**Proverbs 27:23–24 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Leaders drift away from direct knowledge. Information arrives already chewed over: a dashboard, a summary, somebody else's read on how it is going. That feels efficient right up to the moment something fails that the summary said was fine.

Know well the condition of your flocks is a demand for firsthand attention. A shepherd who only hears reports will miss the limp, the thinning coat, the animal that stopped eating. The proverb attaches a reason to the instruction. Riches do not last forever, and a crown does not endure to all generations.

That reason carries weight. Attention is not a one-time inventory. What a leader was handed does not hold its condition on its own. Equipment wears out. Processes rot quietly. Margins erode a point at a time. Good people start looking elsewhere months before they ever say so.

Stewardship requires knowing the real condition of what you hold rather than the reported condition. Those are different numbers more often than leaders expect, and the distance between them is exactly where problems grow without anyone noticing.

Give attention to your herds is unglamorous work. It means walking the floor, reading the raw numbers instead of the summary of them, asking the second and third question rather than accepting the first answer. Nothing entrusted to a leader stays in good condition by itself.

## Practical Application

- Pick one thing you only know through someone else's summary and go look at it directly.
- Ask a second and third question about a number you have been taking at face value.
- Name what has quietly declined under your care while the reports stayed green.

## Takeaways

- Reported condition and actual condition are different numbers, and the gap between them is where failures grow.
- Nothing entrusted to a leader holds its condition without attention.

## Closing Thought

The summary said the flock was fine.
