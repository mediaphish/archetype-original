## Scripture

**Proverbs 23:4–5 (ESV)**

## Reflection

Do not toil to acquire wealth is not a warning against work. It is a warning about the toil that keeps running after the reason for it is gone, when acquiring has quietly become the point and the number has replaced whatever the number was for.

Be discerning enough to desist is an unusual instruction for anyone who has been successful. It assumes a leader can recognize the moment to stop, and that stopping is a skill rather than a failure of nerve. Most of the pressure around a leader argues the opposite.

Then comes the picture. Eyes light on it and it is gone, sprouting wings like an eagle. Whatever a leader has built a sense of security on can leave quickly. Markets turn. Contracts end. One decision made somewhere else can erase a number that took a decade to build.

Stewardship assumes the resource is unstable, which is the reason hoarding it is a poor strategy rather than a cautious one. What is clutched can still fly away. What was put to work at least did something while it was here.

The proverb does not condemn wealth or the people who have it. It questions the toil, the sort that costs a leader years and relationships in pursuit of something that can vanish while they are looking directly at it.

## Practical Application

- Name the number you have been chasing and what it was originally supposed to buy you.
- Decide what enough looks like before the next opportunity arrives to argue with you.
- Put something to work that you have been holding tightly for safety.

## Takeaways

- Knowing when to stop acquiring is a skill, not a loss of nerve.
- What is held tightly can still disappear, so holding is not the safe choice it feels like.

## Closing Thought

It was gone while they were still looking straight at it.
