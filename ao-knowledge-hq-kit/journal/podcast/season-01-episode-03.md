Accountability is easy to talk about. It is much harder to look at the distance between what you preach and how you actually live and find it intolerable.

That is Erik Theis. Thirty two years in public service building second chance systems for other people while quietly running a deficit of his own. Pretrial release. Treatment courts. Juvenile intervention. In his fifties he closed the gap. Quit drinking. Restructured everything around discipline. Built a coaching business out of his own transformation. He wakes up at 3:30 in the morning. Runs a multi-department court operation. Teaches at multiple institutions. And is present with his grandchildren in a way he has clearly decided matters more than any title he has earned. He is not performing reinvention. He did the work.

Adam Theis is his son. Finance and economics degrees from Mizzou. Looked at the problem he wanted to solve, decided he was not equipped, and quietly became equipped. Builds automated freight payment systems by day and a food discovery app called Dishcover entirely on his own in every remaining hour.

This conversation gets into what servant leadership actually looks like outside of a business context. The gap between what you say and how you live. The sacrifice that earns influence. The trust that compounds when someone invests in you without an agenda. The quiet years nobody sees that make the visible years possible. The accountability that changes the outcome of a human life when it is taken seriously.

If you grow people you grow the organization. If you use people you lose it. This episode is proof of the first one.
