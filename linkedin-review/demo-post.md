# The post published on camera

This goes out live during segment 3, so it has to be something you would
genuinely publish. It is a real post, not a test artifact.

## Copy

Most leadership failures are not sudden. They are staged.

The gap shows up first in what does not get said. A standard slips, nobody names
it, and the team reads the silence correctly: this is what is actually enforced.

I write about that gap, and what it costs to close it.

## Notes for the recording

**Keep it short.** You are showing the mechanism, not the writing. A long post
means a long scroll on camera.

**It has a link**, which gives you something to point at when you switch to
LinkedIn and show the post rendered.

**If the post has no engagement yet when you reach segment 4**, that is fine and
arguably better. Point at the zero counts and say that the application shows
totals and nothing else, then switch to LinkedIn itself to show that engagement
data exists there and simply is not something the application requests.

**If you want engagement to show**, publish it an hour before recording and let
a few reactions land. Then segment 4 shows real numbers next to a real LinkedIn
post, which is the strongest version.

**One comment to add on camera**, if you want to show a comment count moving:

> This is the part most teams get wrong.

Add it from your own account on LinkedIn, then return to the application and
refresh the performance view.
