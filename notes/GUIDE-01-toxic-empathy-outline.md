# Guide 1: Toxic Empathy (outline, waiting on Bart)

Status: outline only. Nothing gets written until Bart approves this outline.
Source: Bart's five-part Toxic Empathy series in the journal (March 31 to April 23, 2026). Every idea below comes from those posts. The worksheets are new, built to put his ideas into practice.

Working title: **Holding the Line: A Leader's Guide to Toxic Empathy**
Format: a downloadable guide, about 25 to 35 pages, with fill-in worksheets.
Price: $35 suggested.

## Sections

1. **What toxic empathy is** (from Toxic Empathy)
   - Empathy that has come loose from responsibility.
   - The core question: am I protecting them, or protecting myself from the cost of leading?
   - Worksheet: *The conversations I'm putting off.* Each one, how long it has waited, and the story I'm telling myself about why.

2. **The moment you hesitate** (from Holding the Line)
   - It's almost never a clarity problem. It's a cost problem.
   - The reasons that sound responsible: timing, patience, giving space.
   - Editing yourself in real time: direct statements turning into suggestions.
   - Worksheet: *The softening check.* Write the sentence that needs to be said, then the one you would probably say, and see what got removed.

3. **What it costs the team** (from The Cost of Avoidance)
   - The work doesn't disappear. It moves to the people who keep meeting the standard.
   - Strong people recalibrate. They don't rebel.
   - The question changes from "Is this acceptable?" to "Is this acceptable here?"
   - What is tolerated becomes normal.
   - Worksheet: *Who is carrying it.* Map where the work has shifted on your team.

4. **What servant leadership actually asks** (from Servant Leadership Role)
   - Servant leadership is not soft. It's disciplined.
   - Holding awareness and accountability at the same time.
   - Consistency in the moments when stepping back would be easier.
   - Worksheet: *Context and standard.* For one situation, what is true outside the work, and what the work still requires.

5. **The conversation** (from The Conversation)
   - Define the issue. Don't just reference it.
   - Acknowledge the context without letting it replace the standard.
   - Stay in it until three things are clear: what changes, what that looks like in the work, and what happens if it doesn't.
   - Worksheet: *Conversation planner.* The issue in one sentence, the examples, the expectation, the context to acknowledge, and the next step.

6. **After the conversation**
   - People will test whether the standard was real or just words. That's how teams learn.
   - What you reinforce next matters more than what you said.
   - Worksheet: *30-day follow-through.* What gets named early, what gets credited, and when you check back.

7. **Closing reflection**
   - A few questions to sit with, and one line from Bart to finish.
