# The Room, Revised Edition: change guide

Use this to update the designed book in SamCart. Each change below tells you where to find the text, shows the old text exactly as it appears in the First Edition, and shows the new text to put in its place.

**How to find each spot.** SamCart's editor does not show line numbers, so every change gives you three things:

1. **Where:** the chapter or section, and which paragraph it is (counting from the top of that chapter, not counting headings or section-break lines).
2. **Search for:** the opening words of the old paragraph. Use your browser's find (Command + F) on the editor page, or the editor's own search if it has one.
3. **Old text / New text:** the full paragraph before and after. Replace the whole paragraph unless the change says otherwise.

Paragraph counts can shift slightly if your design splits or merges paragraphs, so trust the search words over the count.

Not included here: the site-only summary and tags at the top of the source file, which do not appear in the printed book.

The leaders who struggled most with unfiltered truth were not the ones running broken cultures. They were the ones who believed, with good reason, that they had built healthy ones. Amy Edmondson's work on psychological safety showed how much a team's willingness to speak depends on whether people feel safe taking that risk. Later work by James Detert and Edmondson went a step further. They found that employees carry unspoken rules about when speaking up is risky, rules like do not embarrass the boss in front of others and do not raise a problem without solid data in hand, and that those rules predict silence beyond what the workplace itself explains. The rest is the management time spent trying to make it work, the drag on the team that had to absorb it, the good people who quietly left while it was being sorted out, the separation itself, and the months spent starting over. The more senior the role, the further that cost travels.

In organizations with high trust, survey data improves. It never becomes fully unfiltered, because the filter is structural, not cultural.
