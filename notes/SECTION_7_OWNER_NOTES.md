## Culture_Science_SECTION_7_PART_15.md

“If we’re calling it the baseline, it needs to be what everything else builds on.”
That is exactly how a statistician would describe it too.
So the clean, statistically sound loop for Year 1 looks like:
Year 1
	•	Q1 – Deep Dive Baseline
	•	10 questions
	•	3 anchors (clarity, trust, stability)
	•	7 rotations designed to give a full picture across all six dimensions
	•	This becomes the official baseline.
	•	Q2 – Standard Pulse
	•	10 questions
	•	3 anchors (same wording, same scale)
	•	7 new rotations (different angles, selected dimensions)
	•	Q3 – Standard Pulse
	•	Same structure: anchors + rotation set C
	•	Q4 – Standard Pulse (Year Reflection Framing)
	•	Same structure: anchors + rotation set D
	•	Wording framed in relation to the year (“Compared to earlier this year…”)
	•	This is where the narrative of the year is extracted:
	•	“Here’s how we’ve moved since Q1 baseline.”
Statistically, this gives you:
	•	Baseline (Q1)
	•	Two mid-year data points (Q2, Q3)
	•	One end-of-year snapshot (Q4)
From that, you can:
	•	calculate slope,
	•	detect non-linear drift,
	•	separate seasonal spikes,
	•	and compute a very robust “year change” metric.
Year 2 and beyond
Every new year, Q1 becomes the new deep-dive-baseline:
	•	It still uses the same anchors and engine,
	•	but now you can compare:
	•	Q1(Y1) → Q4(Y1) → Q1(Y2) → Q4(Y2)
	•	That’s where you get multi-year leadership trajectories, which is where ALI turns into true Institute-level data.

3. So what’s the statistical value of the “deep dive” now?
Given the 10-question constraint and your (correct) instinct about baseline, here’s how I’d define it for the canon:
The deep dive is not a bigger survey. It’s the first, most carefully constructed survey of the year, designed to map all six dimensions clearly enough that every other survey has something solid to compare back to.
Statistically, it adds value because:
	1	It’s where we sample every major dimension intentionally
	•	That first wave touches clarity, trust, communication, safety, stability, and alignment.
	•	Later waves can be narrower because they’re anchored to that complete shape.
	2	It hardens the anchors
	•	Q1 responses to the three anchors define the business’s starting position.
	•	Every later change is computed against that, giving you simple, intuitive change scores: “Clarity anchor is up 12 points since Q1 baseline.” 
	3	It defines the “profile” of the company for the year
	•	We can derive:
	•	leadership profile,
	•	team profile,
	•	dimension strengths/weaknesses,
	•	and early risk patterns.
	•	That profile shapes what we look for in Q2, Q3, Q4.
	4	It lets you separate “we had a bad month” from “we have a systemic issue.”
	•	A single bad quarter doesn’t change the shape of the baseline.
	•	But repeated deviation from baseline does.
	•	Statistically, that’s the difference between noise and signal.
