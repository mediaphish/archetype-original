## SECTION 7, PART XXXIII: THE SEVEN CONDITIONS

ALI measures seven conditions. Each one is something the leader creates. None of them is a personality trait, and none of them is fixed. A leader can strengthen every one of them, starting this week.

Each condition earned its place by passing three tests:
- The leader can change it.
- It is different from the other six.
- When it is weak, the fix is different from the fix for the others.

**1. Clarity: I know what matters.**
Priorities, expectations, and what good looks like are plain enough that people can act without guessing, even when things change.

**2. Consistency: people can predict you.**
The leader does what they say. Their follow-through and tone are steady from week to week, so people don't brace for which version of the leader shows up.

**3. Accountability: standards hold for everyone, the leader first.**
The same expectations apply to everyone. Problems are addressed directly and early. The leader admits mistakes and can be corrected. Care that avoids hard conversations isn't kindness. It's avoidance.

**4. Voice: people can tell you the truth.**
Disagreement, bad news, and early warnings reach the leader without a price attached. When Voice is low, the leader is working from a filtered version of reality.

**5. Growth: people are getting better here.**
The leader develops people, gives real feedback, shares credit, and trusts people with real decisions instead of holding every one.

**6. Support: people are cared for, and pressure is carried fairly.**
People are cared for as people. The load is matched to capacity, the weight doesn't always fall on the same few, and people are strengthened before they break, not after.

**7. Alignment: we are pulling the same direction.**
Work and decisions connect to a shared purpose, and people understand the tradeoffs. Unity without sameness: disagreement is welcome, and drifting apart is not.

**How each condition is measured**
Each condition is measured by two fixed ideas. For Clarity, the two ideas are "I know what's expected" and "it stays clear when things change." Each idea is written four ways, and every round uses a different version, in a shuffled order. The survey feels new each round, but the scores stay comparable from one round to the next. One version of each idea is worded in reverse, which catches anyone who simply agrees with everything.

The leader and the team answer the same ideas, in the same version, in the same order. The team answers about "my leader." The leader answers about themselves. That is what makes the mirror possible.

**The seven will be tested**
After a year of real results, any two conditions that always move together will be merged, and any one that turns out to hold two different things will be split. The list serves the truth, not the other way around.

## SECTION 7, PART XXXIV: THE THREE RESULTS

The seven conditions are what a leader creates. The three results are what those conditions produce. A leader cannot work on a result directly. They work on the conditions, and the results follow.

**Trust: the result of the seven, earned over time.**
Trust can't be declared or demanded. It shows up as candor, as problems raised early, and as people giving their best when no one is checking. When Trust is low, the answer is never a speech about trust. The answer is in the seven conditions.

ALI asks about Trust directly: whether people trust their leader, and whether they have confidence in the leader's judgment. The leader answers by predicting how the team will answer. The gap between the two shows how well the leader reads their people.

**Commitment: people choose to stay and give their best.**
ALI asks whether people see themselves here a year from now. Commitment is a signal, not a verdict. Pay, the job market, and life outside work all play a part. So ALI only ever shows Commitment as a team total, and never when fewer than five team members have answered.

**Drift: the direction the conditions are moving.**
Drift is not a question on the survey. ALI calculates it by comparing each condition with the last round, starting with the second round. Drift is the early warning: it shows that something is sliding while it is still small and still fixable, before it shows up as damage.

Drift is normal. Every culture moves. What matters is whether the leader sees it early and responds.

## SECTION 7, PART XXXV: READING YOUR RESULTS

When a round closes, and once at least five team members have answered, the leader sees their results. The screen is built to answer four questions in plain words.

**1. How is each condition doing?**
Each condition gets a score from 0 to 100, built from the team's answers. The score also comes with a word: Strong, Mixed, or Weak. These cut points are first estimates. They will be adjusted once a year of real results shows where they belong.

**2. Do I see it the way my team sees it?**
This is the mirror. For each condition, ALI shows the leader's own score beside the team's and says one of three things:
- You rate this higher than your team.
- You and your team agree.
- Your team rates this higher.

A gap of ten points or less counts as agreement. A leader who rates themselves well above the team on several conditions is not a bad leader. They are a leader who isn't getting the full picture yet, and now they know.

**3. How sure is this read?**
With fewer than eight team answers, ALI marks the results as an early read. With eight or more, it is a solid read. An early read is still worth acting on. It just deserves a lighter hand.

**4. What do I do next?**
ALI picks one condition to focus on: the one with the lowest team score. For that condition, the leader gets one practical thing to try this week and one sentence they can say to their team to open the conversation. Some conditions also link to a deeper journal post.

The screen also shows:
- one plain headline, such as "Your team sees Clarity as your strongest condition. You rate Voice 14 points higher than they do."
- any named patterns ALI finds (see Part XXXVI)
- from the second round on, how each condition moved since the last round

**How to read it well**
Read the results as a report on conditions, not a verdict on people. Start with where the team and the leader agree, because that is solid ground. Then look at the biggest gap, because that is where the leader's picture is most incomplete. Pick one or two conditions, not seven. Then share what you learned with the team, using Part XXVIII.
