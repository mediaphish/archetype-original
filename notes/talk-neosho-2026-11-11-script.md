# AI and Leadership: full script, draft 1

**Event:** Neosho Chamber of Commerce lunch and learn, Wednesday, November 11, 2026.

**Length:** about 45 minutes, then 15 minutes of questions.

**About this draft:** what you see on screen is in bold. What you say is everything else, written the way you talk. Where it says *[your story]*, put in something only you can tell. I left those open rather than make anything up about you. Say it in your own words. This is a map, not a teleprompter.

## 2. Forget the headlines (5 min)

**SCREEN: Forget the headlines.**
- It is not coming for your job.
- It is not going to end the world.
- It is not magic either.

Raise your hand if you've seen a headline this year that made AI sound like the end of the world.

*(Hands.)*

Now keep it up if you've seen one that made it sound like it's going to do your whole job for you by Friday.

*(Hands.)*

Both of those headlines are selling something. Fear gets clicks. Hype gets clicks. Neither one helps you run a business on Monday morning.

Here's what it actually is. It's a tool. A really strong one. It reads fast, writes fast, finds patterns, and never gets tired. It's also imperfect, and it changes every few months. That's it. Not a monster, not a miracle.

The fear keeps people from trying it. The hype keeps people from using it well, because they expect magic, they get something that needs managing, and they quit.

Both cost you. *(Click.)*

## 3. You're using about 5% of it (5 min)

**SCREEN: You're using about 5% of it.**
- Asking a chatbot a question is the front door.
- There is a whole house behind it.

Hands up if you've used ChatGPT, or something like it.

*(Most hands.)*

Keep it up if it's doing real work in your business every week, without you sitting there typing into it.

*(Few hands.)*

That gap right there is what this talk is about.

Most of us stop at the front door. "Write me an email." "Fix this paragraph." "Give me ten ideas for a post." That's useful. But it's the front door.

The house behind it is work that keeps going when you're not there. Research that's done before you ask. Posts that go out on schedule, in your voice, about your real work. Follow-ups that don't fall through the cracks. Somebody who notices something's broken before your customer does.

I wanted to know how far into that house a small business could actually get. So I built it for myself. *(Click.)*

## 6. I don't trust it (7 min)

**SCREEN: I don't trust it.**
- It is wrong with total confidence.
- My logo was wrong on every card for two months.
- One day it spent fifty dollars talking to itself.

Now that you've seen it work, I need to tell you the most important thing in this whole talk.

I don't trust it.

That's not a knock. It's the job. AI tells you something wrong in exactly the same voice it tells you something right. Same confidence. Same polish. No tells.

Three real examples, all from the last few weeks:

**One.** My logo, the actual Archetype Original logo, was redrawn slightly wrong on every quote card for about two months. The top of the A was cut off. Nobody flagged it. Not the system, not the team. I caught it because I looked.

**Two.** My team raised an alarm that my quote cards were putting words in my mouth, quoting me saying things I never wrote. It was convincing. It was also wrong. The cards were fine. They'd confused a caption with a quote. If I'd just believed them, I'd have pulled good work for no reason.

**Three.** One afternoon the team spent about fifty dollars handing work back and forth to each other. Reviewing each other's reviews. Nobody asked them to. I found it, fixed it, and now there's a hard stop.

*[Optional, your story: the tattoos. It can't draw them right, so every picture of me has my sleeves down.]*

Every one of those got caught because a human was paying attention.

So here's the rule: you don't hand AI the keys and walk away. You lead it. You check its work. Same way you'd treat a sharp new hire who's fast, eager and occasionally, confidently wrong. *(Click.)*

## 7. It never has the whole picture (5 min)

**SCREEN: It never has the whole picture.**
- The data is always incomplete.
- The human factor is always missing.
- The room, the relationship, the judgment call. Those stay yours.

Here's the other limit, and this one doesn't go away with better technology.

It only knows what it's been given. And what it's been given is always incomplete.

It doesn't know your customer just lost his wife. It doesn't know your best employee has been quietly looking for six months. It doesn't know the handshake deal that matters more than the contract, or that the guy who's "not responding" is drowning at home.

*[your story: a time the right call came from something no report would show.]*

Leadership lives exactly in the places the data can't reach: the room, the relationship, the judgment call.

Those stay yours. Don't hand them off. *(Click.)*

## 8. There is no easy button (4 min)

**SCREEN: There is no easy button.**
- It costs real money.
- It takes real time.
- It needs someone watching it. Every day.

I want to be straight with you, because somebody's going to try to sell you an easy button. There isn't one.

What I showed you costs real money every month. It took real time to build. It needs somebody watching it every day: what it says, what it spends, what it gets wrong.

Is it worth it? For me, yes. It does real work while I'm doing other things. But only because I own it. The minute you stop paying attention, it'll happily, confidently drive off the road.

Anybody who tells you it's easy is selling you something. *(Click.)*

---

## 9. Lead it like you lead people (2 min)

**SCREEN: Lead it like you lead people.**
- Set the goal.
- Check the work.
- Keep the human in it.

So here's where I'll land.

The same things that make you a good leader of people make you a good leader of this.
- **Set the goal.** Be clear about what good looks like.
- **Check the work.** Trust, but verify. Every time.
- **Keep the human in it.** The judgment, the relationships, the call: that's yours. Always.

AI won't replace good leadership. It'll expose the lack of it, faster than anything I've ever seen.

Thank you. *(Click.)*
