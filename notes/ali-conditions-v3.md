# ALI conditions, version 3 (approved by Bart 2026-11-16)

Source of truth for the conditions rollout (site, corpus, product, posts). Replaces the
older set (Clarity, Communication, Consistency, Trust, Alignment, Stability, Drift).

Why it changed (2026-11-16 audit): the old list mixed what a leader does with what it
produces (Trust, Stability and Drift are results), Accountability, Bart's most-written
theme, had no home, and Communication overlapped Clarity and Trust. Bart: "I agree drift
is an outcome, not a condition. I agree accountability needs to be there." Seven stays
because each condition passes three tests: the leader can change it, it is distinct from
the others, and it leads to a different fix. After a year of pilot data, conditions that
always move together get merged, and ones that split get split.

## What the leader creates

1. **Clarity: I know what matters.** Priorities, expectations, and what good looks like are plain enough that I can act without guessing, even when things change.
2. **Consistency: people can predict you.** You do what you say. Your follow-through and tone are steady from week to week, so people don't brace for which version of you shows up.
3. **Accountability: standards hold for everyone, the leader first.** The same expectations apply to everyone. Problems are addressed directly and early. The leader admits mistakes and can be corrected. Care that avoids hard conversations isn't kindness. It's avoidance.
4. **Voice: people can tell you the truth.** Disagreement, bad news, and early warnings reach the leader without a price attached. When voice is low, the leader is working from a filtered version of reality.
5. **Growth: people are getting better here.** The leader develops people, gives real feedback, shares credit, and trusts people with real decisions instead of holding every one.
6. **Support: people are cared for, and pressure is carried fairly.** People are cared for as people. Load is matched to capacity, the weight doesn't always fall on the same few, and people are strengthened before they break, not after.
7. **Alignment: we are pulling the same direction.** Work and decisions connect to a shared purpose, and people understand the tradeoffs. Unity without sameness: disagreement is welcome, and drifting apart is not.

## What it produces

- **Trust: the result of the seven, earned over time.** It can't be declared. It shows up as candor, early problem-raising, and people giving their best.
- **Commitment: people choose to stay and give their best.** A signal, not a verdict: pay and the job market play a part too.
- **Drift: the direction the conditions are moving.** Calculated from one round to the next, starting with round two. It's the early warning that something is sliding before it becomes damage. Never asked as a question.

## Patterns ALI can name (combinations)

- High Support, low Accountability: care that avoids hard things (toxic empathy).
- High Accountability, low Voice or Support: standards held through fear.
- Large leader-over-team gaps across conditions: the leader is working from a filtered picture.

## Rollout plan

1. Definitions (done, this file).
2. Survey questions, one condition at a time, two fixed questions per condition plus the results (about 17 questions, about 4 minutes).
3. Rebuild the product around the new set, together with the audit blockers.
4. Site pages (show wording first).
5. Culture Science section 7 (show changes).
6. Posts, through Auto's brief, outline, post stages.

## Questions (Step 2)

Design rules for every question:
- One idea per question, plain words, no jargon.
- Team version says "my leader". Leader version asks about the same behavior from the leader's side.
- Each question is one fixed idea written in four equivalent versions (A to D). Every round draws a different version of each idea, in shuffled order, so the survey feels new while scores stay comparable (Bart's original design: prevent answering from memory).
- One version per idea is reverse-worded and reverse-scored (marked with the reverse sign). It catches people who agree with everything. Rules: leader and team always get the same version (same direction) in the same round; no double negatives; one reversed version per idea.
- The pilot checks that versions of the same idea score alike; one that does not is rewritten or dropped.
- Same five labeled answers for every question: Strongly disagree, Disagree, Neither, Agree, Strongly agree.

### Approved questions

**Clarity** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, I know what's expected
- A. Team: I know what my leader expects of me. / Leader: Everyone on my team knows what I expect of them.
- B. Team: My leader has made it clear what success looks like in my role. / Leader: I have made it clear to each person what success looks like in their role.
- C (reversed). Team: I often have to guess what my leader wants from me. / Leader: My team often has to guess what I want from them.
- D. Team: If someone asked what my leader expects of me, I could answer plainly. / Leader: If someone asked my team what I expect of them, they could answer plainly.

Idea 2, clear when things change
- A. Team: When priorities change, my leader explains what it means for my work. / Leader: When priorities change, I explain what it means for each person's work.
- B. Team: When plans shift, my leader tells me what to stop, start, or keep doing. / Leader: When plans shift, I tell each person what to stop, start, or keep doing.
- C (reversed). Team: When things change, I'm often left unsure what to work on. / Leader: When things change, my team is often left unsure what to work on.
- D. Team: Even when things change quickly, my leader keeps my priorities clear. / Leader: Even when things change quickly, I keep my team's priorities clear.

**Consistency** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, word matches deed
- A. Team: My leader does what they say they will do. / Leader: When I tell my team I will do something, I do it.
- B. Team: When my leader makes a commitment, I can count on it happening. / Leader: When I make a commitment to my team, they can count on it happening.
- C (reversed). Team: My leader often promises things that don't happen. / Leader: I often promise my team things that don't happen.
- D. Team: My leader's actions match what they say matters. / Leader: My actions match what I say matters.

Idea 2, the same person every day
- A. Team: My leader treats people the same way on a good day and a bad day. / Leader: I treat people the same way on a good day and a bad day.
- B. Team: I don't have to check my leader's mood before bringing something up. / Leader: My team doesn't have to check my mood before bringing something up. (Watch: may move with Voice.)
- C (reversed). Team: How my leader reacts depends on what kind of day they're having. / Leader: How I react depends on what kind of day I'm having.
- D. Team: My leader is the same person under pressure as when things are easy. / Leader: I am the same person under pressure as when things are easy.

**Accountability** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, standards hold
- A. Team: When someone isn't meeting expectations, my leader addresses it directly. / Leader: When someone isn't meeting expectations, I address it directly.
- B. Team: My leader holds everyone on the team to the same standards. / Leader: I hold everyone on my team to the same standards. (Watch: fairness may need its own idea.)
- C (reversed). Team: My leader lets problems slide to avoid an uncomfortable conversation. / Leader: I let problems slide to avoid an uncomfortable conversation. (Toxic empathy signal; read with Support.)
- D. Team: My leader deals with problems early, before they grow. / Leader: I deal with problems early, before they grow.

Idea 2, the leader answers too
- A. Team: My leader admits it when they get something wrong. / Leader: I admit it to my team when I get something wrong.
- B. Team: My leader holds themselves to the standards they set for us. / Leader: I hold myself to the standards I set for my team.
- C (reversed). Team: When things go wrong, my leader looks first at what others did. / Leader: When things go wrong, I look first at what others did.
- D. Team: When my leader is wrong, they change course. / Leader: When I am wrong, I change course.

**Voice** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, safe to disagree
- A. Team: I can disagree with my leader without it being held against me. / Leader: My team can disagree with me without it being held against them.
- B. Team: I feel safe telling my leader what I really think. / Leader: My team feels safe telling me what they really think.
- C (reversed). Team: People on my team keep their real opinions to themselves around my leader. / Leader: People on my team keep their real opinions to themselves around me.
- D. Team: When I push back, my leader listens. / Leader: When my team pushes back, I listen.

Idea 2, bad news travels up early
- A. Team: I can bring my leader bad news without paying a price for it. / Leader: My team can bring me bad news without paying a price for it.
- B. Team: If I saw a problem coming, I would tell my leader right away. / Leader: If my team saw a problem coming, they would tell me right away.
- C (reversed). Team: Problems reach my leader late, after they've grown. / Leader: Problems reach me late, after they've grown.
- D. Team: When I raise a concern, my leader tries to understand it before responding. / Leader: When someone raises a concern, I try to understand it before I respond.

**Growth** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, I'm getting better here
- A. Team: My leader helps me get better at my work. / Leader: I help each person on my team get better at their work.
- B. Team: I get honest feedback from my leader that helps me improve. / Leader: I give each person honest feedback that helps them improve.
- C (reversed). Team: My leader doesn't put much into helping me grow. / Leader: I don't put much into helping my team grow.
- D. Team: My leader takes an interest in where I want to go in my career. / Leader: I take an interest in where each person wants to go in their career.

Idea 2, power is shared
- A. Team: My leader trusts me to make decisions in my area. / Leader: I trust my team to make decisions in their own areas.
- B. Team: My leader gives credit to the people who did the work. / Leader: I give credit to the people who did the work. (Watch: may belong with idea 1.)
- C (reversed). Team: Decisions wait on my leader, even small ones. / Leader: Decisions wait on me, even small ones.
- D. Team: My leader gives me real responsibility, not just tasks. / Leader: I give my team real responsibility, not just tasks.

**Support** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, cared for as a person
- A. Team: My leader cares about me as a person, not just as a worker. / Leader: Each person on my team knows I care about them as a person.
- B. Team: My leader notices when I'm struggling. / Leader: I notice when someone on my team is struggling.
- C (reversed). Team: My leader sees me mainly for the work I produce. / Leader: I see my team mainly for the work they produce.
- D. Team: If I had something hard going on outside work, I could tell my leader. / Leader: If someone on my team had something hard going on outside work, they could tell me.

Idea 2, pressure carried fairly
- A. Team: I have what I need to be successful. / Leader: My team has what it needs to be successful. (Bart's original pair; deliberately not "my leader", since resources are partly the company's.)
- B. Team: My leader spreads the workload fairly. / Leader: I spread the workload on my team fairly.
- C (reversed). Team: I don't have what I need to be successful. / Leader: My team doesn't have what it needs to be successful.
- D. Team: When the pressure is on, my leader steps in to help carry it. / Leader: When the pressure is on, I step in to help my team carry it.

**Alignment** (approved 2026-11-16)

Idea 1, I can see how it connects
- A. Team: My leader explains how our work connects to the bigger purpose. / Leader: I explain to my team how our work connects to the bigger purpose.
- B. Team: When my leader makes a tough call, I understand why. / Leader: When I make a tough call, my team understands why.
- C (reversed). Team: I often can't see how my leader's decisions connect to what we say matters. / Leader: My team often can't see how my decisions connect to what we say matters.
- D. Team: My leader is open about the tradeoffs behind big decisions. / Leader: I am open with my team about the tradeoffs behind big decisions.

Idea 2, together, without sameness
- A. Team: My leader keeps our team pulling in the same direction. / Leader: I keep my team pulling in the same direction.
- B. Team: Once my leader makes a decision, the team gets behind it, even people who disagreed. / Leader: Once I make a decision, my team gets behind it, even people who disagreed.
- C (reversed). Team: People on my team are working toward different goals. / Leader: People on my team are working toward different goals.
- D. Team: My leader uses our differences to make better decisions. / Leader: I use my team's differences to make better decisions.

**Trust** (result, approved 2026-11-16). Leader versions predict the team, so the gap shows how well the leader reads their people.

Idea 1, I trust my leader
- A. Team: I trust my leader. / Leader: My team trusts me.
- B. Team: My leader has my best interests at heart. / Leader: My team believes I have their best interests at heart.
- C (reversed). Team: I'm not sure my leader would have my back. / Leader: My team isn't sure I would have their back.
- D. Team: I would follow my leader through a hard season. / Leader: My team would follow me through a hard season.

Idea 2, confidence in their judgment
- A. Team: I have confidence in my leader's judgment. / Leader: My team has confidence in my judgment.
- B. Team: My leader knows what they're doing. / Leader: My team believes I know what I'm doing.
- C (reversed). Team: I often second-guess my leader's decisions. / Leader: My team often second-guesses my decisions.
- D. Team: When things get hard, I trust my leader to make the right call. / Leader: When things get hard, my team trusts me to make the right call.

**Commitment** (result, approved 2026-11-16). One idea, choosing to stay. Shown only as a team total, never below five respondents.
- A. Team: I see myself working here a year from now. / Leader: I expect everyone on my team to still be here a year from now.
- B. Team: If I were offered a similar job elsewhere, I would stay. / Leader: If my team were offered similar jobs elsewhere, they would stay.
- C (reversed). Team: I have thought about leaving in the last few months. / Leader: I expect some of my team have thought about leaving in the last few months.
- D. Team: I plan to keep building my career here. / Leader: My team plans to keep building their careers here.

## Survey shape (decided 2026-11-16)

- Everyone (leader and team) answers the same 17 scored questions: 2 core ideas for each of the 7 conditions, 2 Trust ideas, 1 Commitment idea. One version (A to D) of each idea per round, the same version for leader and team, shuffled order. About 4 minutes.
- Plus 1 unscored trial question per round, drawn from a pool of rotating ideas, to grow and test the bank (Bart: the bank should be deep). A trial idea that proves it moves with its condition can join the core set later.
- Drift is calculated from round-to-round change, starting at round 2.
- Public page changes "ten questions" to "about 18 questions, under five minutes" (Step 4).
- Rotating trial ideas to draft next: Clarity (what good work looks like), Consistency (priorities don't flip-flop), Accountability (fairness), Voice (asked for input before deciding), Growth (recognition), Support (a sustainable pace), Alignment (living the stated values), plus one or two more per condition.
