# Auto workflow audit (2026-10-27)

Bart asked for a 30,000-foot pass after five days of fast building: map the workflows, say what is good and bad as a senior UI/UX researcher, and propose fixes. This is the written copy of what was sent to him in chat.

## The map today

Top tabs: Auto (chat), Library, Queue, Podcast, CRM, Settings.

Still reachable by link, not in the menu: Command Center, Ideas, Scout, Studio, Publisher, Insights, Import, Drafts. Any unknown /ao address falls back to Command Center.

The jobs Bart does, and where each one lives:

| Job | Where it starts | Where he approves | Where he checks it later |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journal post | Chat (a project channel) | Chat panel tabs: Post, Image, Schedule, Captions, Publish | Library, Publish tab |
| Infographics | Made by a weekday job | Queue | Queue "Coming up" |
| Quote cards | Weekly job | Queue | Queue |
| Resurface reshares | Weekly job | Queue | Queue and Library (both) |
| The week's calendar | Vox | Queue (ReachWeek) | Queue |
| Manual posts (LinkedIn Business, Facebook Personal) | Captions | Publish tab | Publish tab, by memory |
| Podcast | Podcast tab, Cam in chat | Podcast tab | Podcast tab |
| CRM | CRM tab | n/a | CRM tab |
| Team work | Chat, Team board | Chat | Team board |
| Settings and engines | Settings (15 sections) | Settings | Settings |

## Good

1. Chat-first group chat with channels: one conversation per project, one bubble per teammate.
2. Approvals are explicit and saved on the post, and the team cannot approve for him.
3. The journal panel shows one thing per tab and follows the real stage.
4. The team checks its own work (Red edits, Ada reviews, huddles catch invented facts).
5. Safety rules are enforced in code, not only in instructions (story rules, AI curtain, photos).
6. Works on the phone, with push notifications.

## Bad (most costly first)

1. Two homes for the same work. Journal posts are approved in chat; infographics, quote cards and reshares in the Queue. Each has its own approve pattern, and "Talk it over" bounces between them.
2. The Queue is a long stack of six different sections with six layouts. It is a place he has to remember to visit.
3. Library, Queue and the Publish tab overlap. Reshares show in both Library and Queue; scheduled posts in three places.
4. Old pages are still alive, and a wrong address lands on the old Command Center.
5. Settings mixes set-once accounts with day-to-day knobs (reshare engine, opportunities, scout sources).
6. What is waiting on Bart is scattered: chat questions, the Team board count, the Queue, push notes.
7. Chat noise: team notes, huddle lines, task cards and system notes sit between the real messages, and long posts pasted into chat get cut off and push questions out of sight ("I can't find the prompt").
8. Many taps per post: six tabs, each its own approval, and the team re-asks.
9. Manual posts have a planned time now, but nothing reminds him at that time.

## Proposed fixes

A. One inbox, in chat. Everything waiting on Bart lands in #today as a card, posted by the teammate who owns it. The Queue page retires.

B. One card for every approval. Preview, the caption(s) with their times, and the same three buttons everywhere: Approve, Change, Set aside. Change opens that item's own conversation.

C. One Calendar page replaces Library and the Queue's week: everything scheduled or published, including the manual posts with a Posted check.

D. Retire the old pages, send unknown addresses to Auto, and split Settings into Accounts (rare) while the daily knobs move into chat ("Vox, pause reshares this week").

E. Quieter chat. Team chatter folds under the answer it belongs to ("3 team notes"), and the one open question to Bart stays pinned above the message box with its buttons. Long posts open in the panel, never pasted in full.

F. Fewer taps. When the schedule and captions are both ready, one "Approve schedule and captions" button, still showing both first.

G. Manual post reminders. A push at the planned time with the caption ready to copy.

Resulting menu: Auto, Calendar, Podcast, CRM, Settings.

Suggested order: A and B together first (they remove the most hunting), then E, then C and D, then F and G.
