# Auto, chat first: the plan

Written overnight 2026-10-25 for Bart's review. Nothing in here is built yet.

## What Bart asked for

- "I don't think we need separate chats for the agents in Auto. Every chat can
  be a full group chat with everyone being able to speak up at will or on
  request. It brings all the experience we have to the table for every
  project. No different than in my company as we self organized around a
  project."
- "Auto is my Slack with my Team."
- "Projects needs to be part of it. I could interact with any of the tabs in
  Auto as a chat."
- "I open a thread and ask for metrics on our quote cards. Auto gives the
  charts. Cam and Reach talk about design and content for social. Jot does a
  post mortem on a journal entry that flopped."
- "In another I could ask for an update on Reshares or Quote Cards so I can
  spot check quality."
- "Auto breaks in and tells me we have a podcast episode blowing up and we
  pull in Reach to see how we can build on it. Two way communication."
- "All in chat. No dashboard. But I could call for a dashboard in chat!"
- "Start ramping up automation and maybe I do get to make this my living
  again."

## The idea in one paragraph

Auto becomes one place where Bart and his team work, the way a company works
in Slack. Work is organized into channels (a project, a topic, a person) and
threads (one question or one piece of work). The whole team is in every
conversation. One teammate answers by default and the others speak up when
they have something real to add, or when Bart calls them in. Anything Auto
can show on a screen today (a chart, the Queue, a person from the CRM, the
week's posting plan, the podcast pipeline) can appear inside a message when
Bart asks for it, live, and he can act on it right there. And the team does
not only answer: when something matters, a teammate starts the thread.

## What a day looks like

The names and numbers here are made up, to show how it would feel.

**7:30 AM, on his phone.** Auto has opened a thread in #today: "Three things
need you. Matt Burgess replied about his session. Two emails Cam wrote are
waiting. Yesterday's quote card is doing three times your usual." Each is a
card with a button: open the reply, approve the emails, see the numbers.
Bart approves both emails without leaving the thread.

**Mid-morning, spot check.** Bart types in #social: "Show me this week's
reshares." Auto posts the five images as a strip with their captions. He
taps one: "This photo doesn't fit the quote." Reach answers in the same
thread, swaps the photo, and posts the new version for him to approve.

**Noon, the numbers.** In #social Bart asks: "How are the quote cards doing
this month?" Auto answers with a chart (reach and engagement by channel,
best and worst cards). Cam says the dark cards outperform the light ones.
Reach says Tuesday noon is the strongest slot. Bart asks Jot why a journal
entry flopped, and Jot answers in the thread with what it thinks happened
and what to try next time.

**Afternoon, the team speaks first.** Auto opens a thread in #podcast: "The
Keri Kirby episode is getting four times the plays of the last three in its
first day." It brings Reach in. Reach proposes two follow-up posts and a
clip; Bart says yes to one, and it lands on the week's plan.

**Evening, a project.** Bart opens #book-launch and asks where things stand.
The channel remembers its goal, what is done, what is next and who owns
each step, so the answer is current without him explaining anything.

## The pieces

### 1. Channels and threads

- **Channels** are where work lives. A few standing ones: #today (what needs
  Bart), #social, #writing, #podcast, #crm, #team. Plus one channel per
  project Bart starts ("the book launch", "the OCC follow-up"), and one per
  person in the CRM when there is real work with them.
- **Threads** are one question or one piece of work inside a channel.
  Replying to a message (his highlight-and-reply already works this way)
  opens or continues its thread instead of burying the channel.
- **A project channel has a pinned card** at the top: the goal, what is
  done, what is next, who owns each step, and the date it is aimed at.
  Teammates update it as they work, so asking "where are we?" always gets a
  current answer.
- **Unread markers** on each channel, and a bold dot when something is
  waiting on Bart, so on his phone he can see at a glance where to look.
- Existing chats are kept and readable. The separate per-teammate chats
  become the history of #team.

### 2. The whole team in every conversation

- **One voice answers by default.** In a project channel it is the
  teammate who owns the project; elsewhere it is Auto. Bart never gets four
  replies to one question.
- **Others speak up only with something real.** After the first answer, a
  quick check asks each other teammate whether they have something that
  changes the answer (a risk, a better idea, a fact from their area). If
  not, they stay quiet. When they do speak it is short and says why.
- **@mentions** bring anyone in, as now. "@team" asks everyone.
- **Teammates can talk to each other** in the thread (Cam to Reach about a
  design), and Bart can step in at any point.
- **Renaming.** Bart said he may rename some of them. Names are a setting,
  not code, so a rename is one change and every past message updates.
- **Background work stays.** A teammate can take something away, work on it,
  and post back in the same thread when it is done or when it needs him.

### 3. Cards in chat (the "dashboard in chat")

Anything that is a screen today can be shown inside a message, live, with
buttons. Bart can ask for it in plain words, and teammates use them when
they answer.

| Card | What it shows | What Bart can do in it |
|---|---|---|
| Chart | Engagement and reach over time, by channel, by kind of post | Tap a point to open that post |
| Post strip | Recent or upcoming posts with their images and captions | Approve, redo, swap photo, set aside |
| Queue item | One thing waiting on him (an email, a graphic, a reshare) | Approve, edit, discard |
| Person | One person from the CRM: next step, last contact, what is owed | Call, text, email, set a next step |
| Week plan | Reach's posting plan for the week | Move a post, fill a gap |
| Podcast | A guest's stage, an episode's plays | Prepare the guest, plan the follow-up |
| Project | The pinned project card | Mark a step done, change an owner |
| Dashboard | Several of the above together ("show me the podcast dashboard") | Everything the parts allow |

Cards are drawn from live data each time they are opened, so a card from
last week shows today's numbers. The real numbers already exist: 366 posted
items have engagement synced every morning, by channel and kind (quote
cards, journal launches, pull-quote cards, reshares).

One gap: podcast plays. Episodes are uploaded to YouTube from Auto, but no
numbers come back yet, so "an episode is blowing up" needs YouTube's view
counts read back each day (the same connection that uploads). Spotify does
not offer its numbers to outside tools, so those would stay in Spotify's own
app.

### 4. The team speaks first

- **What counts as worth interrupting Bart.** A clear bar, written down and
  tuned with him, for example:
  - A post doing well over two times the usual for its kind and channel in
    its first day.
  - Someone in the CRM replied, or a reply is owed for two days.
  - Something waiting on Bart for more than a day.
  - A scheduled post failed, a job stopped running, or something is about to
    go out that he has not approved.
  - A podcast guest's session is tomorrow and the brief is ready.
- **How it arrives.** A new thread in the right channel, with the facts, a
  card, and what the teammate suggests. Urgent ones also send a short email
  (or a phone notification once that is set up), so he does not have to
  keep Auto open.
- **Quiet hours and a daily limit**, so it never turns into noise: nothing
  between 9 PM and 7 AM Central except failures, and the small stuff rolls
  into the morning #today thread instead of its own interruption.
- **Two way.** Every break-in can be answered in the thread ("yes, do it",
  "pull in Reach", "not now"), and the team acts on the answer.

### 5. Ramping up automation

The goal Bart named is a business that runs without him being in every step.
The rule stays the same as tonight: accuracy before autonomy. Each kind of
action moves up one level at a time, only after it has been right while he
watched.

| Level | What happens | Bart's part |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Draft | The team prepares it | Approves every one |
| 2. Spot check | It goes out on its own on a schedule | Sees a sample in the morning thread, can pull any one |
| 3. Report | It runs on its own | Reads a weekly summary |
| 4. Owned | A teammate owns it end to end | Hears only when something is off |

Where things stand now, and the order I would move them:

1. **Quote cards** already post on their own (level 2 to 3). Add the weekly
   numbers card so he sees what is working.
2. **Reshares** (now level 1). Move to level 2 once he has approved a run of
   the new photos with no fixes needed.
3. **Infographics** (level 1). Move to level 2 after a few weeks of clean
   approvals and no borrowed wording caught.
4. **The weekly posting plan** from Reach (level 1). Move to level 2: Reach
   fills the week, Bart sees it on Thursday and can change anything.
5. **CRM follow-ups and team emails** (level 1). These stay at level 1
   longest, because they go to real people in his name. When the accuracy
   check has been clean for a long run, routine ones (a thank-you after a
   call, a reminder before a session) can move to level 2.
6. **Podcast pipeline**: guest research and prep already run on their own;
   scheduling, follow-ups and the episode's social plan join as the team
   proves them.

Each kind of action shows its own track record (how many in a row Bart
approved without changes), so the decision to move it up is based on what
happened, not on a feeling. Moving up is always Bart's call.

### 6. On the phone first

- One column. The channel list is the home screen; a channel opens full
  screen; a thread slides over it.
- Cards fit a phone width and every button is thumb-sized.
- Typing is optional: each break-in and card has its likely answers as
  buttons ("Approve", "Pull in Reach", "Not now").
- The keyboard never covers the send button (fixed tonight for the round
  Archy button).

## The phases

Each phase is usable on its own, so Bart gets value from the first one
instead of waiting for all of them.

### Phase 1: one conversation, the whole team
- Channels and threads in Auto's chat, with the standing channels above.
- Every thread includes the whole team; one answers, others speak up only
  with something real; @mentions and @team.
- Old chats kept; per-teammate chats folded into history.
- Names as a setting, so Bart can rename teammates.
- **Done looks like:** Bart asks one question in #social and gets one clear
  answer, with Reach or Cam adding a line only when it matters.

### Phase 2: cards in chat
- Charts first (quote cards, reshares, journal launches, by channel and over
  time), then post strips with approve and redo, then Queue items, CRM
  people, the week plan, podcast cards.
- "Show me the X dashboard" builds a dashboard card from the parts.
- **Done looks like:** "How are the quote cards doing?" returns a chart in
  the thread, and "show me this week's reshares" returns the images with
  buttons that work.

### Phase 3: the team speaks first
- The interruption bar above, quiet hours, the daily limit, and the morning
  #today thread.
- Read YouTube view counts back each day so podcast episodes can be watched
  like posts are.
- Break-ins as new threads with facts, a card, a suggestion and answer
  buttons; urgent ones also by email.
- **Done looks like:** a post doing well opens a thread with the numbers and
  a suggestion, and Bart answers "do it" from his phone.

### Phase 4: projects
- Project channels with the pinned project card (goal, done, next, owners,
  date), kept current by the team.
- Every tab reachable from chat; the tabs stay for now and fade as chat
  covers them.
- **Done looks like:** "Where are we on the book launch?" gets a current,
  accurate answer with no explaining.

### Phase 5: the automation ramp
- The four levels, per kind of action, with each one's track record visible.
- Move reshares, then infographics, then the weekly plan to level 2 as they
  earn it; team emails last.
- **Done looks like:** most weeks, Bart's part is the morning thread, a few
  approvals and the decisions only he can make.

## Guardrails that do not change

- Nothing goes to a client or guest until Bart approves it, until he moves
  that kind of email up himself.
- Every statement about a person is checked against the record.
- Checks inform and never override Bart.
- Public content never reveals how Bart builds or uses AI.
- No AI-altered photo of Bart is posted without him seeing it.
- Other people's examples are inspiration for format only.
